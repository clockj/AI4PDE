# Track 1 — Model Comparison and Timing Report — 2026-07-29

Env: `realpde` (torch 2.2.2+cu121) · GPU: RTX 4060 8 GB

Reports in this folder are named `report_<YYYYMMDD>.md`.

## Summary

A **1.93M-parameter U-Net trained with plain MSE on real data alone is the best model found**:
83.83 on held-out trajectories, beating the organizers' 50M FNO (83.06) while running ~13×
faster (0.87 ms vs 11.6 ms/window) — and the U-Net's number is a genuine test score while FNO's
is a training score.

The diffusion experiment is a **clean negative result**: PDE-Refiner scores 82.62 against its own
MSE control's 83.80, worse on both rel-L2 and TKE. The refinement objective did not help on this
data.

Generalization gaps are negligible (≤ 0.57 final points), so **overfitting is not the bottleneck**
— the remaining headroom is in TKE and SPS, not in more capacity or more regularization.

## What was measured

Two matched 336-window validation sets, both built with the official protocol
(`sub_s=2` → 32×64, `T_in = T_out = 20`, stride 20), scored with the **official** `scoring.py`:

| set | trajectories | status |
|---|---|---|
| **unseen** | 3750_0, 6300_10, 8850_20, 11400_20, 13950_10, 16500_5, 21600_15, 26700_0 | excluded from our models' training data |
| **seen** | 5025_0, 7575_10, 10125_20, 12675_20, 17775_10, 19050_5, 22875_15, 25425_0 | in our models' training data |

Matched on Re spread (3750–26700 vs 5025–25425) and AoA coverage. Both sets are training data for
the organizers' `sim_real_ft` checkpoints, so **their delta is a difficulty control**.

Inference is batched at B=16 on GPU, with GaussianNormalizer statistics applied on the way in
and out. `time_score` uses the measured per-window wall time; `+band` re-scores SPS with the
best uniform interval half-width from a small grid search instead of the scorer's default
`±5%·|pred|`.

## Timing detail (inference only, fp32, no channels_last/bf16/CUDA-graph tuning)

| model | params | size on disk | B=1 | B=8 | B=32 | time_score B=1 → B=32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FNO | 50.4M | 403 MB / 201 MB fp16 | 15.5 ms | 11.4 ms | 10.8 ms | 87.3 → 89.2 |
| CNO | 8.0M | 32 MB | 57.5 ms | 57.5 ms | 59.8 ms | 78.1 → 77.7 |
| Transolver | 12.5M | 50 MB | 109.1 ms | 106.3 ms | 678 ms | 72.1 → 50.9 |

Parameter count is a poor proxy for latency here: the 50M-parameter FNO is ~5× faster than the
8M-parameter CNO. CNO's custom StyleGAN-derived ops are effectively per-sample and do not
amortize with batch at all. Transolver degrades at B=32 from VRAM pressure on 8 GB.

`time_score = 100/(1+sqrt(t/0.72896))`: 1 ms → 96.4, 10 ms → 89.5, 100 ms → 73.0, 700 ms → 50.5.

## Findings

**0. A small MSE U-Net beats every official baseline.** 1.93M parameters, 12 minutes of training
on `train_real` only, no sim pretraining, and it scores 83.83 on trajectories it never saw
against FNO's 83.06 on trajectories it trained on. It is also ~13× faster (0.87 vs 11.6 ms),
which is where most of its margin comes from: it gives up on rel-L2 (96.1 vs 96.6) and TKE
(75.1 vs 77.9) but gains 7.9 points of `time_score`.

This is the headline. It supersedes the earlier "start from FNO" recommendation: the organizers'
checkpoint is not the strongest starting point, and its 50M parameters and 201 MB fp16 footprint
buy accuracy that the scoring function barely rewards.

**1. Among the official baselines, FNO wins on both axes at once.** Best accuracy on all three
error metrics *and* 5×/23× faster than CNO/Transolver. CNO and Transolver are strictly dominated
— there is no trade-off to weigh.

Likely reason: the flow is quasi-periodic vortex shedding, which a global spectral convolution
with few modes represents directly, while CNO's local anti-aliased conv stack needs depth for
the same receptive field. There are also only ~3.4k real training windows, so a strong inductive
bias beats a data-hungry attention model.

**2. Sim-only pretraining scores below free persistence** (69.0 vs 79.7). All of the baselines'
real-world skill comes from the real fine-tuning stage. Sim is a weak prior, not a data
multiplier. Untested hypothesis for why zero-shot transfer is this bad: sim has an informative
`p` channel while real is `p = 0`, so the sim model leans on an input that vanishes at test time.

**3. DeepONet lands at 80.94 almost entirely on speed, not accuracy.** 0.85M params, 40 epochs,
105 s of training, `train_real` only. Its field accuracy is barely above free persistence
(rel-L2 93.8 vs 93.5) and its MVPE is *worse* (92.3 vs 93.4); what carries it is `time_score`
97.0 at 0.68 ms/window — 17× faster than FNO.

This was predictable from the parameterization. On a fixed output grid a DeepONet reduces to a
rank-*p* linear decoder over a basis shared across all conditions, so it represents the low-rank
mean field adequately but cannot place per-condition small-scale structure — hence TKE 0.933,
barely better than persistence's 1.000. Adding basis functions raises the rank but not the
conditioning on the input window, so this is a ceiling of the architecture here, not of the
training budget (though the run was still improving at epoch 39 and is undertrained).

The useful reading is not "DeepONet is competitive" but **"the time metric is worth more than
most accuracy work."** A model barely better than repeat-the-last-frame sits 2.1 points off the
best baseline purely by being fast. Corollary: FNO's accuracy at DeepONet's latency would score
≈ 84.7 — more than the entire rel-L2 + MVPE headroom combined, from distillation alone.

**4. Our numbers are honest; the official checkpoints' are not.** DeepONet, U-Net and
PDE-Refiner are evaluated on trajectories they never trained on. The `sim_real_ft` checkpoints
were fine-tuned on all of `train_real`, these 8 trajectories included. Combined with the
difficulty control (FNO Δ = +0.03 between the two sets), the U-Net's 83.83 > FNO's 83.06 is
therefore a *conservative* statement of the margin, not an optimistic one.

**5. Overfitting is not the bottleneck.** Every generalization gap is ≤ +0.57 final points. The
models transfer from 74 training trajectories to 8 unseen ones essentially intact. More data at
the same Reynolds numbers, more regularization, or more capacity will buy very little. The
ceiling is set by the metric structure. (Unmeasurable here: the test set's Re 2968 is outside
the training range entirely.)

**6. The accuracy metrics are nearly saturated; the headroom is elsewhere.** The U-Net's
remaining gap to the oracle, in final-score points:

| subscore | U-Net (mse) now | ceiling | final pts available |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TKE** | 75.1 | 100 | **+5.0** |
| **SPS** | 55.0 | 70.8 | **+3.2** |
| rel-L2 | 96.1 | 100 | +0.8 |
| MVPE | 96.4 | 100 | +0.7 |
| time | 96.7 | ~99 | +0.5 |

TKE and SPS are 82% of everything left. Improving the field prediction — where the obvious
effort goes — is worth 1.5 points total. Note that `time_score` is now nearly exhausted too:
having moved to a fast model, further speed work is worth almost nothing.

## Diffusion models: literature survey and model choice

### Why look at diffusion at all

Every MSE-trained model we measured collapses on TKE — FNO 0.569, DeepONet 0.933, persistence
1.000 — because L2 training regresses toward the conditional mean and attenuates fluctuation
amplitude. That is the exact failure mode the generative-PDE literature was built to fix, and
TKE is the single largest block of our remaining headroom (+4.4 final points).

### Literature reviewed

| work | venue | relevance |
|---|---|---|
| [ACDM — Benchmarking Autoregressive Conditional Diffusion Models for Turbulent Flow Simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01745) (Kohl, Chen, Thuerey; [code](https://github.com/tum-pbs/autoreg-pde-diffusion)) | ICML 2024 | Closest task match. Conditions on the current flow field, unrolls autoregressively. Claims the probabilistic formulation "allows for inferring multiple predictions that align with the statistics of the underlying physics", and concedes "traditional architectures are superior in terms of inference speed". |
| [PDE-Refiner: Achieving Accurate Long Rollouts with Neural PDE Solvers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05732) (Lippe et al.) | NeurIPS 2023 | Diagnoses our exact root cause: MSE surrogates suffer spectral bias, reproducing low frequencies faithfully while high frequencies get attenuated. Fixes it with diffusion-style refinement over decreasing noise amplitudes. Validated on Kuramoto-Sivashinsky and 2D Kolmogorov flow. |
| [Uncertainty-aware Surrogate Models for Airfoil Flow Simulations with DDPM](https://github.com/tum-pbs/Diffusion-based-Flow-Prediction) | AIAA Journal | Same geometry family (airfoil). Produces calibrated uncertainty, which would feed the SPS subscore directly. |
| [PDESpectralRefiner](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10711) | 2025 | Spectral-adjustment successor to PDE-Refiner; not tried here. |

### Chosen model: PDE-Refiner

ACDM is the stronger method on turbulence statistics and would be the pick on accuracy alone.
Our scoring makes it unaffordable. ACDM needs tens of denoising steps *per frame*, unrolled over
20 frames. At ~11.6 ms per network evaluation, a conservative 20 steps × 20 frames is ~4.6 s per
window → `time_score` ≈ 26 against FNO's 88.8. That is roughly −12 final points, and TKE is worth
at most +4.4. **No achievable statistical gain repays the sampling cost of a full autoregressive
diffusion rollout under this leaderboard's time metric.**

PDE-Refiner costs **K+1 = 4 forward passes** for the entire 20-frame block, targets the identical
failure mode, and is a change of training objective rather than of architecture. It is the
version of the idea that survives the time metric. Configuration: K = 3, `sigma_min` = 1e-3,
noise schedule `sigma_k = sigma_min^(k/K)`, on a 1.93M-parameter U-Net.

The honest framing of this experiment is therefore not "diffusion vs FNO" but **"how much of
diffusion's statistical fidelity can be bought inside the time budget."**

### Controlled ablation — result: the refinement objective did not help

`--mode mse` and `--mode refiner` share one U-Net, one dataset (74 trajectories) and one training
budget (40 epochs), so the difference isolates the objective from the backbone.

| variant | forward passes | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | ms/win | **final (unseen)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U-Net, MSE objective | 1 | **.082** | **.665** | **.076** | 0.87 | **83.83** |
| U-Net, PDE-Refiner objective | 4 | .099 | .771 | .080 | 1.84 | 82.53 |

**PDE-Refiner is worse on every error metric, including the one it was chosen for.** It costs 4×
the compute and loses 1.30 final points. This is a clean negative result: on this data the
diffusion-style refinement objective is not worth pursuing.

Without this control the earlier reading would have been badly wrong — PDE-Refiner's 82.53 beats
DeepONet's 81.09 and nearly matches FNO, which looks like a success until the matched MSE
control shows the entire gain came from the U-Net backbone, and the refinement objective
actively subtracted from it.

Two candidate explanations, in order of confidence:

1. **Real PIV high-frequency content is largely measurement noise, not physics.** PDE-Refiner's
   premise is that attenuated high frequencies are real signal worth recovering. That holds on
   the clean Kuramoto-Sivashinsky and Kolmogorov-flow simulations it was validated on. Here,
   refining toward low-amplitude high-frequency structure means fitting sensor noise, which hurts
   rel-L2 *and* TKE (noise inflates the target variance in a way the model cannot predict).
2. **Training-budget confound.** The refiner splits its gradient signal across K+1 = 4 branches,
   so only ~1/4 of its updates train the k=0 prediction task, while the MSE control spends 100%
   there. At an equal 40 epochs the refiner is effectively undertrained on the task being scored
   (its loss was still declining at epoch 39: 0.587 vs the control's 0.042, though the two losses
   are not directly comparable). A fair-compute rerun would give the refiner ~4× the epochs.

Explanation 2 means this result should not be read as "PDE-Refiner does not work", only as
"PDE-Refiner did not pay off at equal epochs on real PIV data". Given that TKE moved the *wrong*
way, though, explanation 1 looks more likely, and a 4× longer rerun is low priority.

## Caveats

- **The official checkpoints' numbers are optimistic.** They were fine-tuned on all of
  `train_real`, both validation sets included, so their scores are closer to training than to
  leaderboard performance. Our three models are scored on held-out trajectories.
- **Neither set tests Reynolds extrapolation.** Both sit inside the training Re range
  (3750–26700). The hidden test set includes Re 2968, below everything. No split of `train_real`
  can measure that, so all "generalization" claims here mean "new trajectory, familiar Re".
- **Our models got a weaker recipe, not a stronger one.** Trained on 74 trajectories (vs 82),
  real data only, no sim pretraining, no hyper-parameter search, 12 minutes each. The U-Net's
  win over FNO is therefore not the result of a bigger budget.
- **The PDE-Refiner comparison is equal-epoch, not equal-compute-on-task.** See the ablation
  section: the refiner spends ~3/4 of its gradient signal on noise prediction.
- Timings are single-GPU fp32 on an RTX 4060 with no `channels_last`/bf16/CUDA-graph tuning; the
  evaluation hardware is unknown and probably faster. The ranking is solid, the absolute
  `time_score` values are not.
- Every number here is local. Nothing has been validated against the Codabench leaderboard.

## Recommendation

**Build on the small MSE U-Net (`Track_1/diffusion/pde_refiner.py --mode mse`), not on FNO.**
It is ahead on a fairer evaluation, 13× faster, 1/26th the parameters, trivially under the
256 MB cap, and trains in 12 minutes — which makes iteration cheap. Do not spend time on CNO,
Transolver, DeepONet, or diffusion refinement.

1. **Tune the SPS interval band** — ~1 day, **+1.3 final measured** (83.83 → 85.15), no model
   change at all. Grid-search the half-width and return `lower`/`upper` from `predict()`.
   Verify: `sps_score` > 55.0 with all other subscores unchanged.
2. **Add a variance head for TKE** — the main work, up to +5.0 final. Predict a per-pixel std
   field and rescale the predicted fluctuation to match it. An oracle variance head reaches
   TKE 99.6 on top of a *trivial* mean predictor, so the ceiling here is real.
   Verify: `tke_score` > 75.1 with `rel_l2_score` ≥ 96.0.
3. **Self-normalize inputs by window mean speed** — insurance for Re 2968. Not measurable
   locally; implement on principle (see `CLAUDE.md` "Units").
4. **Do not chase speed further.** At 0.87 ms the U-Net has ~0.5 points of `time_score` left.
   This supersedes the earlier "speed is worth more than it looks" advice, which was written
   when the reference model was FNO at 11.6 ms.
5. **Validate on the leaderboard early.** All conclusions here rest on 336-window local sets
   with no leaderboard confirmation. Spend one daily submission on the U-Net before building
   further on it.
