## 1. The headline: the metric has a low ceiling, and TKE is most of it

The plan targeted TKE for +4 final points on the strength of an oracle experiment. That oracle
read the target. Measuring what is actually *predictable* changes the picture completely.

`Track_1/tke_ceiling.py` (raw output in `tke_ceiling_20260729.txt`) estimates the TKE field with
progressively stronger, increasingly illegal estimators:

| TKE estimator, unseen set | rel-L2 | `tke_score` | legal at inference? |
|---|---|---|---|
| TKE of the 20 **input** frames | 0.804 | 71.3 | yes |
| **U-Net (MSE, 150 ep) — the shipped model** | **0.633** | **76.0** | yes |
| per-trajectory mean TKE field | 0.577 | 77.6 | no (averages the targets) |
| the target's own TKE, 3×3 box-smoothed | 0.284 | 87.6 | no |
| the target's own TKE | 0.000 | 100.0 | no |

Why the floor is where it is: split each 20-frame target window into two 10-frame halves and
compare their TKE fields. They are two independent estimates of the *same* underlying quantity,
and they disagree by **rel-L2 0.937** — implying ≈0.47 irreducible relative error at 20 frames.

This is not measurement error. The lag-1 autocovariance test puts PIV white noise at only
**16.0% (u) / 10.9% (v)** of the scored variance, below the plan's 30% threshold for explicit
noise modelling. The floor is the *sampling* variability of a turbulent variance estimated from
20 frames — it is baked into the target, so no predictor can remove it.

**`tke_score` therefore tops out near 78–81, not 90+.**

### What that does to the achievable total

| subscore | now (unseen, fitted band) | hard ceiling | max remaining final pts |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rel_l2_score` | 96.0 | 100 | +0.80 |
| `tke_score` | 76.0 | ~80 | +0.80 |
| `mvpe_score` | 96.3 | 100 | +0.74 |
| `time_score` | 96.7 | ~99 | +0.46 |
| `sps_score` | 61.8 | **73.1** | +2.26 |

`sps_score` is `100/(1+exp(-v))` where `v` is a weighted mean of terms each ≤ 1, so it can never
exceed **73.13** no matter how good the model or the bounds are. A *perfect* predictor scores
(100+100+100+99+73.1)/5 = **94.4**.

Starting from **85.35** today, everything left on the table is **+9.1 final points**, and that
requires exact prediction of turbulent PIV. A realistic target for this work package is
**87.5–88 local**, i.e. roughly **86 on the leaderboard**. 90 is not reachable through WP1+WP2.

> This also makes the 93.3 at the top of the leaderboard hard to reconcile with the scorer in
> the starting kit: it needs all three field subscores ≥ 99 *and* `sps_score` ≈ 73. Worth
> understanding what that submission is before treating 93.3 as the bar — especially since our
> own leaderboard result came in *below* local, i.e. the hidden set is harder, not easier.

| file | what |

| `Track_1/tke_ceiling.py` | the ceiling measurement above |
| `Track_1/unet/wp_train.log` | raw training logs for every run |

`UNetTwoHead` differs from the shipped `UNet` in two ways:

1. **No `z` slot, no step embedding** (WP1.3). The refinement machinery was dead weight for
   MSE-style training and doubled the cost of the first convolution. 1.93M → 1.91M parameters,
   and measurably faster.
2. **An optional per-pixel log-std head** (WP2), a 1×1 convolution branching off the *same*
   final decoder feature map as the field head — ~130 extra parameters. The heads share
   everything up to the last decoder block on purpose: fluctuation intensity is set by the
   large-scale flow, so the variance readout is conditioned on the same latent that encodes the
   mean field. The separation happens in the latent, not in two independent networks.

At inference the fluctuation is renormalized to carry the predicted std:

```
m = mean_t(y);  f = y - m;  s = std_t(y)
y_out = m + f * (alpha*sigma_hat + (1-alpha)*s) / s.detach()
```

`alpha = 0` is the identity, so the composition cannot regress the plain-field baseline.

### The loss (WP1.1)

The field term is per-sample relative L2 **in physical units**:

```
||(pred - y) * STD|| / ||y * STD + MEAN||       per sample, then averaged
```

Computing it in normalized units would be a different objective — the normalized denominator is
dominated by the constant `MEAN/STD` offset, which erases exactly the low-Re up-weighting that
WP1.1 exists to produce.

The TKE term is written directly as `0.5 * sum_c (sigma_hat_c * STD_c)^2` rather than by calling
`tke(compose(...))`, because with `alpha = 1` those are identical and the direct form keeps the
gradient on the variance head alone. It is computed in **physical** units: `STD_u/STD_v = 6.1`,
so a normalized TKE would over-weight the v fluctuation by 37×.

**+0.30 final on unseen.** Where it came from is not where the plan expected:

- **rel-L2 did not improve on unseen** (0.083 → 0.084) despite the loss now being the scored
  metric. On *seen* it improved sharply (0.060 → 0.049). The relative-L2 objective bought
  memorization, not generalization.
- The gain is **TKE** (0.633 → 0.599, +1.0 subscore) and **speed** (96.7 → 97.2 `time_score`,

- **The generalization gap widened from +1.61 to +2.49.** Overfitting is now the binding

That last point redirected the rest of the work: it motivated run E below (amplitude/noise
augmentation), which is not in the original plan.

### SPS bands (WP3.1/3.3, fitted on seen, reported on unseen)

| band | fitted | `sps_score` | final |
|---|---|---|---|
| scorer default ±5%·\|pred\| | — | 55.23 | 84.32 |
| constant half-width | 0.0060 | 61.95 | 85.66 |
| `c` × per-element RMSE map | 1.50 | 61.99 | 85.67 |
| `c` × optimal `h(RMSE)` | 0.80 | **62.33** | **85.74** |

A half-width proportional to the local error is no better than a flat constant. The reason is in
the integrand: each element contributes `exp(-(u-l)/sigma)` when the target is inside, so for
Gaussian error of std `s` the expected contribution is `exp(-2h/sigma)·erf(h/(s√2))`. Its optimum
**saturates near `sigma/2`** for badly-predicted elements instead of growing with `s`. Using that
rule (`optimal_half` in `eval_wp.py`) recovers a further +0.38 `sps_score`.

A per-element **bias map** fitted on seen and applied to unseen changed nothing (85.66 vs 85.66)
— the model's errors are not systematically located.

### The variance head works; the training scheme broke the trunk

Two facts have to be held apart.

**The head itself is a clear success.** On unseen it predicts the per-pixel temporal std at
**corr 0.904, rel-L2 0.363**, against the trivial input-window-std estimator's 0.756 / 0.559 —
the plan's stated bar, cleared by a wide margin. And the composition does what it was designed
to do: sweeping `alpha` from 0 to 1 moves TKE error monotonically 1.533 → 0.569, exactly the
knob the plan predicted.

**The failure is elsewhere: the field head degraded.** Compare like for like against WP1:

| | WP1 `wp1_s5` | WP2 `wp2_s5` | |
|---|---|---|---|
| rel-L2 (unseen) | 0.084 | 0.111–0.124 | **worse at every alpha** |
| MVPE (unseen) | 0.079 | 0.106 | **worse, and alpha-independent** |
| train field loss | 0.049 | 0.103 | |

MVPE is the diagnostic. `compose` preserves the temporal mean exactly, so MVPE is *identical*
for every `alpha` — 0.106 across the whole sweep. It therefore cannot have been damaged by the
composition. It was damaged by **training through** the composition.

The cause: with `alpha = 1` the field loss sees `m + f·sigma_hat/std(f)`, so the magnitude of
the raw fluctuation `f` is divided straight back out and never constrained. The trunk gets no
gradient telling it to get the fluctuation amplitude right, and it drifts — which is why the raw
field at `alpha = 0` has TKE error **1.533**, worse than predicting no fluctuation at all. The
trunk spent capacity on a degree of freedom the loss had made invisible.

The plan anticipated a rel-L2 cost from renormalization and put `alpha` in as the safety valve
against it. That valve does not cover this failure, because the damage is baked into the weights
at training time: `alpha = 0` does **not** recover the WP1 model, it recovers a *worse* one
(79.20). The safety-valve argument in the plan is therefore wrong as written — it holds only if
the composition is applied at inference and not trained through.

### The fix — run F, `--alpha-train 0`

The field head trains on the raw field exactly as in WP1; the variance head still trains on
`L_var + L_tke` and still shares the trunk, so the latent coupling holds. The composition is
applied only at inference, with `alpha` swept.

`wp2f_s5.pth`:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | unseen | 0.084 | 0.670 | 0.081 | 96.0 | 74.9 | 96.1 | 96.1 | 54.9 | **83.59** |
| 0.25 | unseen | 0.085 | 0.623 | 0.081 | 95.9 | 76.2 | 96.1 | 97.1 | 54.9 | **84.05** |
| 0.50 | unseen | 0.086 | 0.575 | 0.081 | 95.9 | 77.7 | 96.1 | 97.1 | 54.9 | **84.34** |
| **0.75** | **unseen** | 0.089 | 0.544 | 0.081 | 95.7 | 78.6 | 96.1 | 97.1 | 54.8 | **84.49** |
| 1.00 | unseen | 0.093 | 0.561 | 0.081 | 95.6 | 78.1 | 96.1 | 97.2 | 54.7 | **84.33** |
| 0.75 | seen | 0.071 | 0.256 | 0.051 | 96.6 | 88.7 | 97.5 | 97.2 | 55.5 | **87.07** |

Best band at `alpha = 0.75`: `c` × optimal `h(RMSE)` → `sps_score` 61.78, **final 85.88**.

**The decoupling works, and WP2 is worth +0.14 over WP1 — inside run-to-run noise (±0.1).**

Point by point:

- **The field head is recovered.** At `alpha = 0`, rel-L2 is 0.084 and MVPE 0.081, matching WP1
  (0.084 / 0.079). Training through the composition was indeed the whole problem.
- **The variance head is again excellent**: corr **0.907**, rel-L2 **0.358** on unseen.
- **`alpha` now behaves as the plan intended.** It is a genuine trade: TKE 0.670 → 0.544 as
  rel-L2 goes 0.084 → 0.089. The sweep picks 0.75, not 1.0 — partial rescaling wins, because the
  last quarter costs more rel-L2 than it returns in TKE.
- **But the return is tiny.** Against WP1: tke +1.6, rel −0.3, mvpe −0.1, sps −0.55, net **+0.14**.

There is also a real cost to sharing the trunk: at `alpha = 0` run F's raw TKE error is 0.670
against WP1's 0.599. The variance losses reshape the shared latent and the field head pays for
it. Runs H and I test whether blocking that (`--detach-var`, keeping the head's *read* of the
mean-flow latent but not its gradient) recovers the difference.

### Why WP2 cannot pay more than this

The variance head is a technical success the metric does not reward. It predicts the per-pixel
std at corr 0.91 — but §1 already showed the target's own TKE field carries ≈0.47 relative
sampling noise, so converting a good `sigma_hat` into a good TKE *score* is capped. Run F sits at
TKE 0.544 against that ≈0.47 floor. There are roughly **2 subscore points** left in TKE, i.e.
**+0.4 final**, for any amount of further work on the variance head.

The plan projected +2 to +4 final for WP2. The correct figure, measured, is **+0.1 to +0.5**.
The error was in the plan's oracle experiment, which used the target's own per-pixel std and so
measured the value of *knowing* the answer rather than the value of *predicting* it.

### Run H — the shared latent has to be *shaped*, not just *read*

Your requirement was that the variance separate from the mean **in the latent space**, because
in a real flow the fluctuation comes with the large-scale flow. Run H tests the weakest possible
version of that: `--detach-var` lets the log-std head read the same final decoder feature map,
but blocks the variance losses from back-propagating into the trunk. The head is still
conditioned on the mean-flow latent; it just cannot change it.

| | run F (trunk shared) | run H (trunk detached) | input-std baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sigma_hat` corr on unseen | **0.907** | 0.679 | 0.756 |
| `sigma_hat` rel-L2 on unseen | **0.358** | 0.635 | 0.559 |
| best alpha | 0.75 | **0.00** | — |
| final | **85.88** | 85.74 | — |

**Detaching destroys the variance head.** At corr 0.679 / rel-L2 0.635 it is *worse than the
trivial estimator that just copies the input window's std*, and the alpha sweep correctly turns
the composition off entirely — H is WP1 with a dead head bolted on, and scores exactly WP1's
85.74. Its training losses say the same thing: variance loss plateaus at 1.36 and TKE loss at
0.83, against F's 0.28 and 0.21.

So the coupling you asked for is real and it is load-bearing — but it runs in the direction that
is easy to miss. A 1×1 convolution on a latent optimized purely for the mean field cannot read
the fluctuation intensity out of it. The variance head only works when its loss is allowed to
*put* fluctuation information into the shared trunk. Sharing the representation is necessary;
sharing the gradient is what makes it work.

(Run H also required a fix: the log-std head is now initialized to a uniform, roughly correct
std. With a default init the TKE loss starts around 1e2 and drives `logstd` into its clamp,
where the gradient is exactly zero and the head never recovers. This only surfaced once the
trunk could no longer bail the head out.)

## 5. Augmentation — no effect (run G, not in the plan)

WP1 widened the generalization gap to +2.49, so overfitting became the obvious next target.
`wp1_s5_aug.pth` adds two augmentations to the WP1 recipe:

- **amplitude scaling**, log-uniform in [1/1.25, 1.25]. In physical units this is `u -> a*u`,
  which to first order is a different Reynolds number — the exact shift the hidden test set makes
  (it contains Re 2968, below the whole training range).
- **input noise** at 0.5× the window's own fluctuation std.

| model | set | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | best band | **final** | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wp1_s5` | unseen | 0.084 | 0.599 | 0.079 | 62.33 | **85.74** | +2.49 |
| `wp1_s5_aug` | unseen | 0.084 | 0.613 | 0.079 | 62.30 | **85.66** | +2.32 |

**No gain.** The gap narrowed slightly (+2.49 → +2.32) but unseen performance did not move: the
augmentation regularized the *seen* score down rather than lifting the unseen one. Note this does
not test the thing it was built for — neither validation set contains an out-of-range Reynolds
number, so its value as insurance for Re 2968 is still unmeasured and unmeasurable locally.

| `c` × `sigma_hat` | 2.60 | 59.78 |
| `c` × optimal `h(sigma_hat)` | 1.30 | 60.72 |
| **ORACLE — width chosen per element with hindsight** | — | **64.92** |

The oracle row is the answer to the whole question. Choosing every half-width *knowing the
actual error* scores 64.92; the best legal rule scores 61.88. **The entire remaining value in
band engineering is 3.0 subscore points, +0.61 final, and no legal method captures all of it.**

Two rules of thumb that turned out to be wrong, and why:

- **`h` proportional to the local error does not work** (61.22, worse than a flat constant). Each
  element contributes `exp(-(u-l)/sigma)` when the target is inside, so for Gaussian error of
  std `s` the expected contribution is `exp(-2h/sigma)·erf(h/(s√2))`, whose optimum **saturates
  near `sigma/2`** instead of growing with `s`. Applying that rule (`optimal_half`) recovers
  +0.56.
- **`sigma_hat` is the wrong quantity for the band** (59.78–60.72, worse than the RMSE map). It
  predicts the flow's *fluctuation*, not the model's *error*. They correlate, but the band needs
  the latter.

Per-window amplitude scaling of the map — correcting for the fact that the sets span Re
3750–26700 and absolute error scales with velocity — is worth only +0.10.

A per-element **bias map** fitted on seen and applied to unseen changed nothing on any model.
The errors are not systematically located.

| `wp2k_w64_b16` — width 64 control | 1.91M | 0.0658 | 0.179 | **86.14** |
| `wp2i_s5` — width 64, batch 32 | 1.91M | 0.0675 | 0.188 | **86.12** |

**More capacity fits the training data better and generalizes slightly worse**, and costs
`time_score` (97.4 vs 98.2) on top. Batch 16 vs 32 makes no difference (86.14 vs 86.12).

This is the cleanest statement of the diagnosis: train field loss 0.066 ≈ seen rel-L2 0.049,
against unseen 0.084. The model already fits what it is shown. The constraint is the **74
trajectories**, not the 1.9M parameters.

## 8. What is actually left, measured

Every remaining subscore now has a measured bound rather than an estimated one.

| subscore | best now | measured bound | how the bound was obtained | max final gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tke_score` | 78.6 | ~81 | 10-frame half-window split ⇒ ≈0.47 irreducible | **+0.5** |
| `sps_score` | 61.9 | 64.9 | oracle band, widths chosen with hindsight | **+0.6** |
| `rel_l2_score` | 95.7 | ≤98.5 | PIV white-noise norm ⇒ rel-L2 floor 0.031 (loose) | +0.6 |
| `mvpe_score` | 96.2 | 100 | not bounded | +0.8 |
| `time_score` | 98.2 | ~99 | 0.4 ms/window forward at batch 16 | +0.2 |

Realistic total from 86.12 is **~87.5**, i.e. roughly **86 on the leaderboard**. The 90 target is
not reachable: `sps_score` cannot structurally exceed 73.13, and a *perfect* predictor scores
94.4. Nothing in WP1–WP3 changes that.

### The one lever with real headroom left

rel-L2 and MVPE are the only subscores whose gap is **generalization** rather than a noise floor:

| | seen | unseen |
|---|---|---|
| rel-L2 | 0.049 | 0.084 |
| MVPE | 0.032 | 0.079 |

The model class can already reach 0.049 / 0.032 — it just cannot do it on trajectories it has not
seen. Six recipes (loss, stride, augmentation, capacity, batch, two-head) all left unseen rel-L2
pinned at 0.084–0.089. That is a **data** limit.

**Recommendation: test sim pretraining of the trunk.** It is the only substantial untested lever
and the only one that adds trajectories. `report_20260729.md` established that sim-only training
scores 69.0 — *below* free persistence — so sim is a weak prior for direct prediction, and that
result was used to rule sim out. But it does not rule out pretraining the trunk on sim and
fine-tuning on real: those are different claims, and the plan's own "out of scope" note flags
trunk pretraining as untested. Expected value is uncertain; it is the only idea left with a
plausible +1.

Explicitly do **not** spend more effort on: SPS band forms (3.0 subscore points exist and none is
reachable), the variance head (within ~2 subscore points of the sampling-noise floor), denser
windows, augmentation, or capacity — all measured flat or negative above.
