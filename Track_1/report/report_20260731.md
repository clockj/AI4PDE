## 1. Where the error actually lives (`diag_spectral.py`)

Time-mean and fluctuation are orthogonal under the time inner product, so
`||P−Y||² = T·||mean_t(P)−mean_t(Y)||² + ||f_P−f_Y||²` splits the rel-L2 exactly.
On `val_unseen`, `wp2i_s5` at `alpha = 0.75`:

| predictor | rel-L2 | = mean part | ⊕ fluctuation part |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wp2i_s5` | 0.0888 | **0.0503** | 0.0729 |
| input time-mean | 0.1338 | 0.1017 | 0.0842 |

Two facts follow, and both were unexpected.

**The target's fluctuation is only 8.4% of the field norm.** So a predictor with a *perfect*
time-mean and *zero* fluctuation would score rel-L2 0.0842 — which is **better than the model's
0.0888**. Every point of fluctuation skill the model has is more than cancelled by its
time-mean error.

**The model captures 16.5% of the fluctuation energy** and 68% of its squared error sits in the
fluctuation half. The gradient budget is being spent where the return is smallest.

### 1.1 How much of the fluctuation is reachable at all

Spectral coherence of the fluctuation, per radial wavenumber (`sqrt(kx² + 4ky²)` on the 32×64
grid, where `dx == dy` and the domain is 2:1, so both axes reach Nyquist at k = 32).
Coherence is phase agreement independent of amplitude; gain is amplitude independent of phase.

| k | energy % | coherence | gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 9.3 | 0.645 | 0.999 |
| 3 | 10.5 | **0.786** | 0.983 |
| 4 | 20.0 | 0.690 | 0.952 |
| 6 | 10.6 | 0.511 | 0.904 |
| 8 | 5.7 | 0.384 | 0.850 |
| 12 | 2.7 | 0.272 | 0.752 |
| 16 | 1.5 | 0.183 | 0.678 |
| 24 | 0.5 | 0.094 | 0.634 |
| 32 | 0.4 | 0.077 | 0.535 |

The flow is quasi-periodic vortex shedding, and it shows: coherence peaks at **0.786 at k = 3**,
the shedding scale, and 57% of the fluctuation energy sits in k ≤ 6 where coherence is 0.5–0.8.
Phase *is* partly predictable at the energy-containing scales. It collapses below 0.2 above
k ≈ 15, but those wavenumbers carry under 8% of the energy between them.

Energy-weighted, **Γ² = Σ E_k γ_k² = 0.308**. That is the hard ceiling on how much fluctuation
energy *any* per-wavenumber linear filter of this prediction can recover. The model is already
at 16.7%.

### 1.2 Per-wavenumber gain correction — rejected analytically, no GPU spent

Gain is already ≈1.0 at k ≤ 3 (that is `compose` doing its job), and falls to 0.5–0.7 at high k.
The tempting move is to boost the high-k gain back to 1. It is wrong: with coherence γ, the
L2-optimal multiplier is **γ**, not 1, so the correct move at high k is to *shrink*, not boost.

Optimizing the per-k gain against measured `(E_k, γ_k, gain_k)`:

| gain rule | fluctuation err² (÷ target energy) | variance kept | rel-L2 | Δ final |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| current (`compose`, α = 0.75) | 0.833 | 0.782 | 0.0886 | — |
| best per-k **at fixed variance** | 0.800 | 0.782 | 0.0874 | **+0.013** |
| Wiener optimum (`b_k = γ_k`) | 0.692 | **0.308** | 0.0833 | +0.050 |

The Wiener row buys +0.05 final by throwing away 69% of the fluctuation variance, which would
send `tke_score` through the floor. The variance-preserving row is worth **+0.013**, an order of
magnitude below run-to-run noise. **Closed.**

### 1.3 What this kills, and what it points at

This closes the residual-reconstruction line discussed at the start of the session:

- A second network cannot beat Γ² = 0.308 by re-filtering the existing prediction, and reaching
  even that costs all of TKE.
- Recovering *new* coherent fluctuation means beating the U-Net at phase prediction with the same
  74 trajectories and the same information. Capacity is already measured non-binding (width 96
  scored 86.01 against width 64's 86.14).
- FNO is the worst available choice for it regardless: its spectral convolution truncates modes
  (baseline `modes (4,12,16)`), so it is a low-pass operator being proposed for a high-pass job,
  and it costs 15.5 ms/window against the U-Net's 0.4 — **−2.2 final before any gain**.

It pointed instead at the **time-mean** — 0.0503 of error that looked learnable, driving
`mvpe_score`, the largest non-SPS headroom on the board (96.2 → 100, +0.76 final). That
hypothesis is wrong, and §1.4 is the measurement that kills it.

### 1.4 The time-mean is already at its sampling floor (`diag_mean_floor.py`)

The scorer compares against the target's *realized* 20-frame mean, and 20 frames spans only a
few shedding periods, so that realized mean is the true mean flow plus a partially averaged
fluctuation. Applying the same half-window trick that produced the TKE floor — split each target
into two 10-frame halves with means `m1, m2`; for near-independent halves the 20-frame mean
carries sampling error `0.5·(m1 − m2)` in rms:

| | value |
|---|---|
| `wp2i_s5` mean-part rel-L2 | **0.0503** |
| irreducible sampling floor of the target's own 20-frame mean | **0.0531** |
| reachable | **0.0000** |

**The model's time-mean is already as good as a predictor that knows the exact true mean flow.**
It is nominally 0.003 *past* the floor, which is not an error in the measurement: the model has
coherence 0.786 at the shedding scale, so it predicts a little of the target window's specific
phase and therefore a little of that window's specific sampling excursion. Frames are
positively autocorrelated, so the halves are not fully independent and the true floor is if
anything slightly higher than 0.0531.

The same measurement at the wake probes:

| | MVPE |
|---|---|
| `wp2i_s5` | 0.0791 |
| a perfect-true-mean predictor | **0.0670** ← the floor |

So `mvpe_score` headroom is 96.20 → 96.75, worth **+0.11 final** — not the +0.76 that assuming a
ceiling of 100 implied. There is no mean-field lever. Improving the time-mean further is the
*same* phase-prediction problem as the fluctuation, not a separate easier one.

### 1.5 The trajectory's own history (`diag_history.py`)

`predict()` receives every test window at once in `(filename, time_id)` order, and windows tile
each trajectory contiguously. So for window *k* the frames of windows 0..*k*−1 are **inputs** —
legally available, no target read. That is a strictly larger conditioning set than the 20 frames
the model currently sees: a median of 430 frames, up to 840.

| estimator of the target window's per-pixel 20-frame std | rel-L2 | corr |
|---|---|---|
| input window std (20 frames) | 0.501 | 0.823 |
| trajectory history std, causal | 0.531 | 0.869 |
| `wp2i_s5` variance head | **0.358** | **0.907** |

On its own, history is **worse** than the 20-frame window and does not improve as it
accumulates (0.560 at 40–160 frames, 0.522 at 420–840). The flow is not stationary enough for a
long average to describe the next 20 frames, and the local window shares low-frequency
modulation with the target that the long average erases. Same for the mean: history mean gives
MVPE 0.103 against the model's 0.079.

**But it carries information the head does not have.** Least-squares combinations, all
evaluated against the target std:

| features | sigma rel-L2 |
|---|---|
| head alone | 0.3577 |
| head + window | 0.3460 |
| head + history | 0.3394 |
| head + window + history | **0.3376** |

**Finding trajectory boundaries at submission time.** History needs to know where one trajectory
ends and the next begins. `metadata["sample_id"]` should give it, but a fallback exists: the
relative jump in per-window mean streamwise speed is 0.046 at most *within* a trajectory and
0.108 at least *across* a boundary on `val_unseen` — a 2.4× separation, 0 errors out of 335 at a
0.077 threshold. Prefer `sample_id`; the threshold is insurance. Note the margin is not huge and
the hidden set has unseen Reynolds numbers, so two adjacent test trajectories could sit closer
together than any pair here does.

## 2. Experiments

`train2.py` gains four flags this round, all no-ops at their defaults. The two used in this
section (the decomposition identities behind `--w-mean` are verified exact in float64):

- `--w-mean` weights the time-mean half of the field loss. `1.0` reproduces the previous loss
  bit-for-bit; `>1` moves gradient from the fluctuation (68% of the budget, ≤31% recoverable)
  to the mean (32% of the budget, not noise-limited).
- `--lam-p` adds the scorer's own MVPE quantity as a loss term — 4 probe columns × 9 rows,
  time-averaged `u,v`, per-column relative L2, transcribed from
  `scoring.mvpe_rel_l2_per_sample`. 36 points carry a fifth of the final score.

The other two, `--hist` and `--lam-f`, belong to §2.5. Base recipe for all runs is
`wpA2_mse_var_lo` (MSE field loss, variance head, λv 0.3 / λt 0.15, stride 5, 150 epochs,
batch 32) — the best-attributed configuration from `report_20260730.md`.

> **Result in one line: the loss is not the constraint.** Both reweightings — toward the
> time-mean, and toward the scorer's own probe functional — landed inside noise. Together with
> the six recipes of `report_20260730.md` that left unseen rel-L2 pinned at 0.084–0.089, that is
> eight attempts to move a quantity that §1.4 now shows is already at its floor.

| run | change | prediction after §1.4 | rel-L2 | MVPE | **final** | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wpA2_mse_var_lo` | control | — | 0.0912 | 0.0806 | 86.08 | — |
| `wpE1_wm3` | `--w-mean 3` | neutral or worse | 0.0909 | 0.0822 | 86.12 | null, +1.2σ |
| `wpE3_lp03` | `--lam-p 0.3` | ≤ +0.11 | 0.0919 | 0.0827 | — | null, −0.8σ |
| ~~`wpE2_wm8`~~ | `--w-mean 8` | worse than E1 | — | — | cancelled | — |

**E3: adding the scorer's own MVPE quantity to the loss does not improve MVPE** — 0.0827 against
the control's 0.0806. Per §2.4 the three control seeds span 0.0806–0.0835, so this is a **null
result, not a regression**; an earlier draft of this report called it "worse", which the noise
measurement does not support. The point stands either way: optimizing the scored functional
directly buys nothing, because the model is already within 0.014 of the probe-point sampling
floor (0.0670) and extra gradient can only fit 36 locations on 74 trajectories.

`wpE2_wm8` was cancelled once E1 and E3 agreed: a second point on a dead axis was not worth
40 minutes of GPU.

**E1 confirms §1.4.** Tripling the weight on the time-mean half of the loss left rel-L2 at 0.091
and MVPE at 0.082 (control: 0.091 / 0.081) — no movement in either quantity it was built to
improve, and +0.05 final, well inside noise. The variance head is also unchanged (sigma rel-L2
0.356 vs 0.358). The time-mean really is at its floor; there was nothing for the extra gradient
to buy.

Note the generalization gap grows monotonically with alpha (+2.09 / +2.60 / +3.12 at
0.50 / 0.75 / 1.00), because the variance head fits the seen trajectories' TKE far better
(seen `tke_score` 90.2 at alpha 1.0 against unseen 78.4). This is a property of the head, not of
`--w-mean`.

On sequencing: §1.4 was measured *after* these runs were launched, and predicted they would do
nothing. They were left running as a **falsification test** of that measurement — had
`--w-mean 3` pushed the mean-part error below 0.0503, the floor estimate would have been wrong
and §1.4 would have needed redoing. It did not, so the floor stands.

### 2.2 The PIV noise fraction is smaller than previously reported

`report_20260730.md` puts PIV white noise at **16.0% (u) / 10.9% (v)** of the scored temporal
variance, from a lag-1 autocovariance test. Re-measuring on `val_unseen` targets: white noise
contributes to the lag-0 autocovariance only, but the *signal* also decorrelates between frames,
so differencing `a0 − a1` charges that decorrelation to noise. Extrapolating `a1, a2` back to
lag 0 instead gives:

| method | u | v | rel-L2 floor from noise |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a0 − a1` (previous report) | 16.0% | 10.9% | 0.031 |
| linear extrapolation from `a1, a2` | **0.8%** | **0.1%** | **0.007** |

Both are biased in known directions — the first up, the second down if the true autocovariance is
peaked at lag 0 — so the honest reading is a range, 0.6%–13% of fluctuation energy. **No
conclusion changes, and two get stronger:** the noise is even further below the 30% threshold
that would have justified explicit noise modelling, and the ≈0.47 TKE floor is even more clearly
sampling variability of a turbulent variance rather than a measurement artifact.

What it does change is the composition of the rel-L2 floor. It is **not** noise-dominated —
0.0531 of it is the time-mean sampling term from §1.4 and at most 0.031 is noise, giving a total
floor of 0.0535–0.0614 and a `rel_l2_score` ceiling of 97.0–97.4.

### 2.3 Post-hoc sigma calibration — **+0.16 final, no retraining** (`calib_sigma.py`)

The §1.5 combination can be applied to an already-trained checkpoint: three scalars and an
intercept per channel, a handful of elementwise ops, no weight touched and no measurable
forward cost. The only question is where the coefficients are fitted.

**Fitting them on `val_seen` does not work, and the failure is instructive.** The model trained
on those trajectories, so its head is in-sample there and already near-perfect; least squares
duly assigns it all the weight (`u: +0.982 head, +0.006 window, +0.000 history`) and the fit
does not transfer — sigma rel-L2 0.3603 → 0.3584, `tke_score` +0.04. This is the same trap as
fitting the SPS band on `seen`, but sharper, because the quantity being calibrated is the
model's own output.

Cross-fitting **within** `val_unseen` (fit on 4 trajectories, apply to the other 4, both ways)
is the honest measurement:

| sigma | alpha | sigma rel-L2 | rel-L2 | TKE | `tke_score` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| head | 0.75 | 0.3603 | 0.0888 | 0.5461 | 78.55 |
| head | 1.00 | 0.3603 | 0.0922 | 0.5579 | 78.19 |
| cross-fit calibrated | 0.75 | **0.3421** | 0.0888 | 0.5255 | 79.19 |
| **cross-fit calibrated** | **1.00** | **0.3421** | 0.0924 | **0.5163** | **79.48** |

Net against the head's best: `tke +0.93`, `rel −0.15` ⇒ **+0.16 final**. Note the optimal alpha
moves 0.75 → 1.00: a better sigma deserves more trust, exactly as `compose` was designed to
allow.

**For a submission the coefficients must be fitted on trajectories the model never trained on.**
`val_unseen` qualifies — it is out-of-sample for a 74-trajectory checkpoint and the hidden test
set is out-of-sample for both, so fitting on all 8 and applying to the hidden set leaks nothing.
The cost is that `val_unseen` then stops being a clean reporting set for this one component;
the +0.16 above is the cross-fit estimate of what it buys.

### 2.5 The decoupled pair — the round's one real gain

`report_20260730.md` §9 left one option explicitly untested: *"a detached head with more
capacity — several conv layers on `u1.detach()`"*. This goes further and makes the variance
predictor a **separate network** (`--lam-f 0` trains one), paired at inference with a frozen
field model. Errors on `val_unseen`, best alpha:

| config | sigma rel-L2 | rel-L2 | TKE | **MVPE** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wpA2_mse_var_lo` — shared trunk, control | 0.3593 | 0.0912 | 0.5470 | 0.0806 |
| `wpE4_hist` — history channels in training | 0.3552 | 0.0900 | 0.5380 | 0.0816 |
| `wpA0` field + `wpF1` sigma | 0.3521 | 0.0880 | 0.5198 | **0.0773** |
| **`wpA0` field + `wpF1` sigma + calibration** | **0.3378** | **0.0880** | **0.5061** | **0.0773** |

`wpF1_varonly` is a width-32 `UNetTwoHead` with the history channels, trained on the variance
losses alone — 0.48M parameters, 14 minutes.

**Two things happen at once, and both are wins.**

1. **MVPE returns to the no-head control's 0.0773, by construction.** The field network is
   `wpA0_mse_nohead`, untouched, so the variance objective cannot reshape its trunk. This is the
   exact failure that cost ~1.3 mvpe on the hidden set.
2. **The separate net predicts sigma *better* than the shared head** (0.3521 vs 0.3593), and
   0.3378 after calibration.

Point 2 **inverts run H's conclusion.** That run detached the head and found it collapsed to
corr 0.679, below the trivial input-std estimator, and concluded the variance losses must be
allowed to reshape the shared trunk. But run H's detached head had **130 parameters** — a single
1×1 convolution. Given an actual network the result reverses: sharing the trunk was never
necessary, it was compensating for a head with no capacity. The previous round drew a
qualitative conclusion from what was really a capacity ablation.

### Where the gain comes from

All rows use `wpA0_mse_nohead` as the field model, best alpha (0.75 throughout), against the
3-seed control mean of 270.37 (σ 0.20, §2.4):

| sigma source | sigma rel-L2 | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | sum3 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| shared trunk (control) | 0.3608 | 0.0913 | 0.5425 | 0.0817 | 270.37 | — |
| `wpF2` variance-only, **no** history | 0.3607 | 0.0881 | 0.5244 | 0.0773 | 271.29 | +0.92 |
| `wpF2` + calibration | 0.3440 | 0.0884 | 0.5120 | 0.0773 | 271.66 | +1.29 |
| `wpF1` variance-only + history | 0.3521 | 0.0880 | 0.5198 | 0.0773 | 271.44 | +1.07 |
| **`wpF1` + calibration** | **0.3378** | 0.0880 | **0.5061** | **0.0773** | **271.86** | **+1.49** |

| component | Δ sum3 |
|---|---|
| decoupling the variance net from the field net | **+0.92** |
| history channels into the variance net | +0.15 |
| post-hoc calibration (§2.3) | +0.42 |

**The decoupling is 62% of the gain, and `wpF2` is the sharpest evidence for it**: a width-32
dedicated net matches the shared-trunk head's sigma to three decimals (0.3607 vs 0.3608) while
MVPE drops 0.0817 → 0.0773 and rel-L2 0.0913 → 0.0881. The shared trunk bought *nothing* in
variance quality and cost both field subscores. Every previous round paid that price.

The calibration's contribution shrinks from +0.78 on the shared-trunk model (§2.3) to +0.42
here, because `wpF1` already consumes the history internally — the two overlap, as expected.

## 3. The stride-20 tiling: 93.55 local, and it explains the leaderboard

The official windowing uses `IN_STEP = OUT_STEP = INTERVAL = 20`
(`realpde_t2_starting_kit_v4/.../local_eval.py:48-51`). Window *k* therefore covers input frames
`[t, t+20)` and target frames `[t+20, t+40)`, while window *k+1* covers input frames
`[t+20, t+40)` — **the same frames**. Verified bit-exact on `3750_0.h5`:

```
frames 868 -> 42 windows
window k target vs window k+1 input:  identical: True | maxabsdiff 0.0
last window's target available in inputs?  False
```

Track 1's `predict(input_array, metadata)` receives **all N test windows at once**, in
`(filename, time_id)` order. So for 41 of every 42 windows the correct answer is another row of
the array that was handed in. On `val_unseen` (336 windows, 8 trajectories, 328/336 = 97.6%):

| | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | sps | **final** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wp2i_s5` alone | 0.0888 | 0.5460 | 0.0791 | 54.81 | 84.70 |
| + tiling structure | **0.0023** | **0.0141** | **0.0021** | 70.47 | **93.55** |
| + tiling, oracle band | 0.0023 | 0.0141 | 0.0021 | 72.93 | 94.05 |

**93.55 against the leaderboard's 93.3.** For comparison, the arithmetic requirement for 93.3 is
that `rel_l2`, `tke` and `mvpe` all sit at ≈98.4 *and* `sps` sits at its structural maximum of
73.11 — while PIV white noise alone (16.0% u / 10.9% v of the scored variance) floors rel-L2 at
0.031 and TKE error near 0.13, capping a physically perfect forecaster around 92. No forecasting
result reaches 93.3. This one does, to within 0.25.

### Status of this route

Undecided, and it is not a technical question. Points on both sides:

- The contract does not forbid reading other rows of the input array, and transductive inference
  over a test batch is ordinary practice.
- Track 2 reveals the previous window's target *explicitly*, one window at a time, batch 1, in
  strict order. The organizers know about the tiling; Track 1's batched call looks like an
  oversight, and could be patched (shuffled windows, or a per-window call) at any time.
- Winners open-source two weeks before the presentation.

**Action taken: none yet. Recommended first step is to ask the organizers on the competition
forum whether cross-window information is permitted.** It costs no submission slot and decides
whether any further modelling work is worth doing.

### The part of this that is unambiguously legitimate

`predict()` is given no Re or AoA, but it *is* given every window of every test trajectory at
once. Estimating each trajectory's freestream speed, Reynolds number, shedding frequency and
variance statistics from all ~42 of its windows and conditioning on them is ordinary test-time
adaptation, reads no target, and is the natural fix for the Re-extrapolation problem that
`--norm self` was built for. Worth doing regardless of how the question above is answered.

## 4. Packaging the decoupled pair

Three things the current `Track_1/submit/` does not do, all required by §2.5:

1. **Ship two checkpoints and run both.** `wpA0_mse_nohead.pth` (7.65 MB) for the field,
   `wpF1_varonly.pth` (1.96 MB) for sigma — 9.6 MB total against the 256 MB cap, no issue.
   `compose(field, sigma, alpha=0.75)` at inference.
2. **Compute the history channels inside `predict()`.** `wpF1` takes them. They need trajectory
   boundaries: use `metadata["sample_id"]` when present, else the mean-speed jump test (§1.5;
   0 errors out of 335 locally at threshold 0.077, but the margin is not large and the hidden
   set has unseen Reynolds numbers, so prefer `sample_id`).
3. **Ship the calibration coefficients** — 8 floats, `[head, window, history, const]` per
   channel.

**Where to fit those coefficients is the one real decision.** They must come from trajectories
the *model* never trained on, or least squares hands the head all the weight and the fit does
not transfer (§2.3, measured). Two options:

| | pro | con |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) ship the 74-trajectory models, fit on the 8 held out** | no leakage, coefficients honestly out-of-sample, and the hidden set is out-of-sample for both | gives up 10% of the training data |
| (b) train on all 82, reuse coefficients fitted from the 74-models | keeps all data | the coefficients no longer match the shipped weights |

**Recommend (a).** The 8 extra trajectories are worth perhaps +0.1 by the round-2 all-data
comparison, which is below transfer noise, while (b) introduces a mismatch of unknown sign in
the one component that actually gained this round.

## 5. Summary

### What moved

| change | Δ final | σ | ship it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| decouple the variance net from the field net (§2.5) | +0.15 | — | **yes** |
| post-hoc sigma calibration on window + history std (§2.3) | +0.16 | — | **yes** |

### What was closed, with the measurement that closed it

| direction | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| residual / high-frequency reconstruction | dead | Γ² = 0.308 caps any per-k filter; best variance-preserving gain +0.013 (§1.2) |
| FNO for the residual | dead twice over | mode truncation is a low-pass; 15.5 ms costs −2.2 final before any gain (§1.3) |
| time-mean as a separate lever | dead | already at its sampling floor, 0.0503 vs 0.0531 (§1.4) |
| explicit MVPE probe loss | null | 0.0827 inside the control spread 0.0806–0.0835 (§2, §2.4) |
| loss reweighting generally | null | three variants, all ≤1.2σ (§2) |
| in-training history channels | superseded | marginal sigma gain, costs MVPE; post-hoc calibration is better (§2.5) |
| more capacity, anywhere | dead | width 96 lost for the field net, width 64 loses for the variance net (§2.5) |

### Two prior conclusions corrected

1. **Run H's "the variance head must share the trunk" was a capacity artifact.** Its detached
   head had 130 parameters. A width-32 network detached from the same trunk *matches* the shared
   head's sigma exactly (0.3607 vs 0.3608) and costs nothing in MVPE — and with history it beats
   it. The shared trunk was pure downside all along, and it is the most likely cause of the
   −1.28 mvpe on the hidden set.
2. **`mvpe_score`'s ceiling is 96.75, not 100**, and PIV noise is 0.6–13% of fluctuation energy,
   not 16%/10.9%. The remaining forecasting envelope is ~+1.1 from here, not the ~+4 implied by
   bounding subscores at their metric maxima.

### Recommended next actions

1. **Ask the organizers whether cross-window information is permitted** (§3). It costs no
   submission slot and it determines whether any further modelling work has a point.
2. **Package `wpA0_mse_nohead` + `wpF1_varonly` + calibration** per §4, fitting the calibration
   on the 8 held-out trajectories and shipping the 74-trajectory models.
3. **Do not** spend further effort on: the loss function, the fluctuation field, model capacity,
   SPS band forms, or the time-mean. All are measured flat against floors established by the
   data.
