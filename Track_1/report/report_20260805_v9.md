# RealPDE Track 1 — the v9 rule change, and what is left to win

2026-08-05. Supersedes the scoring sections of `report_20260804_state.md`; the modelling
sections there still stand.

---

## 1. Summary

The organizers replaced the scoring program (kit v7 → v9) and the hidden test set. The board
returned **79.733** for `Track_1/submit/submission.zip`, the July 30 U-Net package.

Two changes did essentially all of the damage, and both are in SPS:

| | v7 | v9 |
|---|---|---|
| `score_sps(w)` | `100 / (1 + exp(-w))` | `100 * w` |
| elements with `target == 0` | counted, and scored at the maximum | dropped from numerator **and** denominator |

The aggregate `w` the model produces barely moved across the rule change — **0.376 → 0.369**.
The visible collapse from `sps 59.30` to `sps 36.94` is the rescaling, not a regression.

The consequences run the other way from how the number looks:

* SPS used to be clamped between 50 and 73.11 whatever we did. It is now a full 0–100
  subscore, so it is simultaneously **the weakest subscore and the one with the most room**.
* Its slope went from ~23 per unit of aggregate to exactly 100. **Every improvement that
  reduces per-element error is now worth 4.4× more through SPS than it was**, on top of what it
  is worth through rel-L2, TKE and MVPE.

`final_score` is no longer published and is no longer the plain mean of the five: the board's
own five subscores average 78.999 against a reported 79.733. One equation in five unknowns is
not solvable, so everything below tracks **mean5**, the plain mean, and flags it as a proxy.
All five subscores still move `final_score` monotonically, so it remains a valid ranking signal.

**Status:** `submit_fno/submission.zip` is rebuilt against v9 and validated SUBMITTABLE. It
carries the three all-data seeds, with the band fitted from the held-out models' error field on
the AoA 15–20° subset. Projected **~81**, clearing the target of 80.

The local column is **contaminated**: this package's checkpoint is `wp2i_alldata`, trained with

report and it is why §6 below rejects several locally-positive changes.

### Timing

`score_time(t) = 100/(1+sqrt(t/0.72896))` inverts to **5.975 ms/window** on the board for a
package that takes 2.648 ms here. The FNO package's previous board run implied 1.351 ms against
1.390 ms local — a ratio of 0.97. The two are irreconcilable as one machine, so the v9
evaluation is running on hardware roughly **2.3× slower** than the v7 evaluation did.

The FNO should therefore be budgeted at ~3.1 ms on the board (`time_score` ≈ 93.8), not the
1.35 ms its last board run showed. That is a 2-point swing and it is the reason §5 spends any
effort on latency at all.

## 3. The v9 aggregate factorizes — and that is what made the rest of this possible

Writing `pm` for a sample's normalized error metric, v9's aggregate is

```
w = (1/n_scored) * sum over scored (i,e) of  a_i * exp(-2*h_e/sigma) * 1[|E_ie| <= h_e]
a_i = 0.5*(1-pm_dm_i) + 0.3*(1-pm_tke_i) + 0.2*(1-pm_mvpe_i)
```

The three branches differ **only in a per-sample factor**, while the interval term is
per-element. `sps9.py` implements this and checks it against the kit's own `aggregate_sps`
(agreement 1.5e-5). Two things follow.

**Scoring a band becomes one pass instead of three.** A band sweep that took minutes takes
seconds, which is what made §4 affordable.

**The band problem separates over elements, and each element is solved exactly.** For a fixed
element the objective is a decreasing exponential times a step function, so its maximum sits
exactly at one of the observed `|E_ie|`. Sorting solves the whole field in one pass — no
Gaussian assumption, no grid. `sps9.optimal_band`.

That exact solution is also the **ceiling on band fitting**, which is what finally closed the
question of whether more band work was worth doing.

## 4. The band: solved, and nearly exhausted

Fitted on `val_seen`, reported on `val_unseen`, 3-seed `fno_grad` ensemble at `ALPHA = 0.75`:

| band | median h | seen | unseen |
|---|---|---|---|
| scorer's own default ±5%\|p\| | — | — | 22.77 |
| constant | 0.00681 | 48.86 | 47.73 |
| `optimal_half(c·RMS)` — the incumbent | 0.00713 | 50.22 | 48.95 |
| error quantile, best of q = 0.4…0.8 | 0.00618 | 48.87 | 47.52 |
| **exact per-element optimum** | 0.00677 | **51.54** | **49.54** |

The exact solver is worth +0.59 over the incumbent and, more usefully, its `seen` column is the
**upper bound for any band of this form**. There is no third parametric family worth trying.

### Sizing it for the board rather than for here

The band is the only part of the submission fitted directly against an error distribution, so
it is the only part that breaks when the distribution shifts — and board errors run 1.46×
local. Rows are the scale a band was built for, columns the scale it is met with:

| built for | median h | met 1.0 | 1.2 | 1.4 | 1.6 | 1.8 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 0.00677 | **49.54** | 45.22 | 41.32 | 37.81 | 34.67 | 31.87 |
| 1.4 | 0.00825 | 49.06 | 45.36 | **41.97** | 38.87 | 36.03 | 33.45 |
| 1.6 | 0.00889 | 48.60 | 45.10 | 41.90 | **38.97** | 36.27 | 33.80 |
| 2.0 | 0.01005 | 47.54 | 44.36 | 41.45 | 38.78 | 36.31 | **34.04** |

This table is the one that ended the band work. Building for the wrong scale costs **~0.7 SPS**;
the error scale itself costs **8.2**. The shipped band is built for 1.5×, near the measured
1.46, and the row is flat enough that the exact choice does not matter.

### …and then §7b changed the fit split, which was worth more than the inflation

A uniform 1.46× stretch is a crude model of the shift: it widens the interval everywhere,
including the low-incidence pixels where errors are genuinely small and a narrow interval is
worth a lot. Once §7b established that the board resembles the **AoA 15–20° subset** (rel-L2
0.1082 against the board's 0.1179, a 1.05× extrapolation instead of 1.46×), the band could be
fitted on the error field the board actually presents rather than on a stretched average of one
it does not. SPS on `val_unseen`, three views:

| band | median h | all | AoA 15–20 | AoA 15–20 ×1.05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all `val_seen`, inflate 1.0 | 0.00677 | **49.54** | 42.56 | 41.20 |
| all `val_seen`, inflate 1.5 | 0.00860 | 48.82 | 43.24 | 42.09 |
| **hi-AoA `val_seen`, inflate 1.1** | 0.00870 | 47.85 | **43.78** | **42.63** |

**+0.54 on the best proxy.** The two bands have almost the same median half-width (0.00860 vs
0.00870) — the gain is entirely in *where* the width is put, which is why the downside on the
"all" view is bounded and why this is a low-risk bet rather than a wager on the AoA mix. It is
also not a wager the "all" view can win: the board's 0.1179 is above our hardest single
trajectory (0.1143), so **no mixture of the AoA regimes we have can reproduce the board at
all**, and the high-AoA view is the only one in the right neighbourhood.

This is the shipped configuration: `fit_band.py --fit-aoa 15 20 --inflate 1.1`.

Total available from intervals, all of it now taken: **~1.9 SPS ≈ +0.38 mean5**.

### Where the remaining SPS actually is

An oracle allowed a half-width per window *and* element — told each individual error — scores
**65.37** against our 49.54, and the accuracy factor alone caps everything at 76.03. So ~16
points sit in knowing which windows are hard. §6 shows the obvious way to get at that does not
work.

## 5. Latency: profiled, and mostly floor

`predict()` on 336 windows, per window:

| | ms/window |
|---|---|
| host→device | 0.047 |
| 3 forward passes + fluctuation rescaling | 0.695 |
| `.cpu().numpy()` + strided host write | 0.251 |
| `pred - band`, `pred + band` | 0.390 |
| `isfinite(pred).all()` | 0.066 |
| **total** | **1.390** |

The **0.109 ms/window "forward pass"** quoted throughout the older reports is one member's GPU
compute and nothing else. It is 8% of the real cost. The API — three (N,20,32,64,3) float32
arrays, 165 MB each — is most of the rest.

Two findings and one rejected idea:

* **The `isfinite` scan is removed.** It cost 0.066 ms/window to duplicate a check the scorer
  already performs, and ours was strictly worse: the scorer degrades a non-finite prediction to
  zero scores, while our `assert` would raise out of `predict()` and fail the run outright. The
  band's finiteness and sign are now established once at import, where the array is 0.5 MB.
* **`pred ± band` costs 0.390 ms fresh and 0.033 ms into a reused buffer** — a 12× gap that is
  first-touch page faults, not arithmetic. Reusing buffers across calls would recover 0.32
  ms/window, ~23% of the whole call.
* **That reuse is rejected.** The kit states `predict` may be called more than once. If the
  harness holds references and concatenates at the end rather than consuming each result, a
  reused buffer silently corrupts every earlier call. The prize is ~+0.3 `time_score`, about
  +0.06 mean5; the downside is a zeroed submission. Not a trade worth making.

Ensemble size, re-checked under v9 (SPS on `val_unseen`, own band each):

| members | sps | Δ | forward cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 47.62 | — | 0.11 ms |
| 2 | 48.59 | +0.97 | 0.22 ms |
| 3 | **48.82** | +0.23 | 0.33 ms |

A fourth member would buy under +0.1 SPS for 0.11 ms. **Three stays.**

## 6. Six things that did not work

Recorded because each one closes a line of attack, and three of them looked good on paper.

**ALPHA is still 0.75.** The fluctuation rescaling buys TKE and pays in rel-L2 *and* in
per-element error. v9 made that third cost 4.4× more visible, which is exactly the kind of
change that moves an optimum — so it was re-swept with the band refitted at every point. It did
not move: mean5 runs 82.83 / 83.03 / 83.24 / 83.45 / 83.63 / **83.74** / 83.71 across
α = 0.00 … 0.90. SPS does peak earlier (49.79 at α = 0.45 against 49.54 at 0.75) but not by
enough to outrun TKE.

**Blending the model's time-mean toward the input window's mean: null, and badly so.** MVPE
averages over all 20 output frames, so it is purely a test of the predicted mean flow — and it
is the worst-transferring subscore, 1.9–2.4× local across every submission. The wake mean
evolves slowly, so carrying the input window's mean forward should be a lower-variance
estimator with nothing to generalize. It is not: MVPE goes 0.0698 → 0.1240 on `val_unseen` as
the blend moves from all-model to all-input, monotonically, with rel-L2 and SPS following. The
model's mean is simply much better than persistence. (Substituting the *true* mean sends MVPE
to exactly 0 and rel-L2 only from 0.0807 to 0.0673, confirming MVPE is a pure mean-flow metric
and that ~70% of rel-L2 error is fluctuation.)

**Ensemble spread as an adaptive band: null.** With the oracle 16 points above us, member
disagreement is the standard free proxy for which windows are hard. It correlates with |error|
at 0.484 overall but only **0.347** once the per-element mean is removed — and only that
residual is new information, since the band already varies with (t, y, x). The best variant,
`static × ratio^0.25`, scores 50.04 on unseen against the incumbent's 49.54 but **49.4 versus
51.5 on seen**, i.e. it is worse in-sample and better out-of-sample. That sign flip is noise,
not a result, and shipping it would cost ~0.3 ms/window because the bounds would have to be
built on the GPU and transferred.

**A fitted fluctuation gain on top of ALPHA: null, and a textbook overfit.** ALPHA rescales each
pixel's fluctuation toward the variance head's per-window prediction, but the head is trained on
a log-std loss rather than on what TKE measures, so a static multiplicative gain fitted offline
should mop up any systematic bias — and it would be free, folding into the existing GPU compose.
Fitted on `val_seen`, reported on `val_unseen` (mean5):

| | seen | unseen |
|---|---|---|
| baseline, ALPHA = 0.75 | 84.348 | **83.554** |
| scalar g = 1.075 | 84.465 | 83.570 |
| per-pixel g(y,x,c) | 84.533 | 83.519 |
| per-pixel/time g(t,y,x,c) | 84.401 | 83.540 |

Every form gains in-sample and two of three *lose* out-of-sample. The variance head is already
well calibrated and there is no systematic residual to correct.

**Input-noise augmentation: null at both strengths.** One candidate cause of the 1.46× board
inflation is that the model is tuned to the noise level of its training trajectories. Perturbing
the input window by a fraction of the target's own fluctuation std (mean5 on `val_unseen`,
single-member): baseline seeds **83.200 / 83.274 / 83.177**, `aug-noise 0.05` → **83.163**,
`aug-noise 0.15` → **83.181**. Both inside the seed spread and on the wrong side of it.

Note the seed spread here — 0.097 across three seeds — is larger than the 0.015 quoted in
earlier reports. That 0.015 was the σ of v7's `final`, whose logistic SPS compressed exactly the
part of the variation that v9 exposes. **Run-to-run σ in v9 mean5 is ~0.05**, so the bar for a
real single-model result is ~0.15, not 0.04.

**The `--inflate` ordering trap, avoided rather than hit.** Inflating the error map before
fitting the scale `c` would drive `c` down by the same factor and cancel the entire correction.
`c` has to be fitted at the local scale where it can be scored, then applied to half-widths
re-solved at the inflated scale. The v9 rewrite drops `c` entirely — the exact solver has no
scale parameter — so the trap is now structurally absent.

## 7. The loss weights the wrong channel — and that turns out to be correct

`--loss mse` runs in **normalized** units, so a 1σ error in v costs the same as a 1σ error in u.
With σ_u/σ_v = 6.1 that weights v about **37× more than u per unit of physical error**.

Nothing the leaderboard measures agrees:

| | u | v |
|---|---|---|
| physical RMS error | 0.01392 | 0.00563 |
| target std | 0.11535 | 0.01426 |
| relative error | **12%** | **39%** |
| share of rel-L2 error energy | **85.9%** | 14.1% |
| share of target energy | 99.4% | 0.6% |
| SPS contribution (of 38.0 max each) | 21.93 | 26.89 |
| band coverage | 0.832 | 0.930 |

rel-L2 and MVPE are physical norms. SPS measures every interval against one global σ in
physical units. All three are dominated by u; the loss is dominated by v.

The reading that makes this worth testing rather than merely noting: **v is already the far
more heavily weighted channel and is still the worse-predicted one, by 3×.** That is the
signature of a channel sitting near its own noise floor, not one starved of capacity — v is the
small transverse component, mostly fluctuation, and PIV resolves it worst. If so, taking weight
off v costs little and gives u room.

`train2.py --chan-pow p` interpolates: weights are `(std_c / max std)^p`, so `p = 0` is the old
loss exactly and `p = 1` is physical units. (`--loss rel` was already physical; only the MSE
branch is affected.)

**Falsified, with a monotone trend** (mean5 on `val_unseen`, single member):

| p | 0 (3 seeds) | 0.25 | 0.5 | 1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean5 | 83.200 / 83.274 / 83.177 | 83.254 | 83.116 | **83.010** |

p = 0.25 is inside the seed spread; past that the score falls monotonically, and full physical
units is the worst configuration tested. So the "misalignment" is not one. The most likely
reading is that v's weight is earning its keep through TKE and the vortex structure — v is
almost entirely fluctuation, and TKE is a variance metric that does not care about u's
dominance in absolute magnitude — and that the loss and the score are weighting different
things *on purpose*. **The existing weighting stands.**

## 7b. Error is governed by angle of attack, not Reynolds number

Per-trajectory rel-L2 for the 3-seed ensemble, all 16 validation trajectories:

| AoA | trajectories | rel-L2 |
|---|---|---|
| 0° | Re 3750, 5025, 25425, 26700 | 0.0485 / 0.0578 / 0.0556 / 0.0677 |
| 5° | Re 16500, 19050 | 0.0436 / 0.0452 |
| 10° | Re 6300, 7575, 13950, 17775 | 0.0683 / 0.0713 / 0.0774 / 0.0770 |
| 15° | Re 21600, 22875 | 0.1020 / 0.1044 |
| 20° | Re 8850, 10125, 11400, 12675 | 0.0981 / 0.1013 / 0.1059 / 0.1143 |

Reynolds number spans 7× across this table and explains essentially nothing; **AoA 15–20°
carries 2.4× the error of AoA 5°**, monotonically from 5° up. Physically unsurprising — high
incidence means separated flow and stronger, less periodic shedding — but it had not been
measured here before, and it reframes the board gap.

The training set is near-uniform in AoA (17 / 14 / 18 / 17 / 16 trajectories at 0 / 5 / 10 / 15
/ 20°), and both validation splits inherit that mix. Weighting the per-AoA errors above by the
training distribution predicts a same-distribution rel-L2 of **0.077**; `val_unseen` measures
0.0807, so the splits behave exactly as the AoA composition says they should.

**The board measures 0.1179 — higher than our worst single trajectory (0.1143).** No reweighting
of the AoA mix we have can produce that number. So the hidden test set is not a resample of
these 82 trajectories: it is at conditions outside this grid (higher incidence would fit the
trend), or from separate experimental runs with different PIV noise. That is consistent with the
other standing puzzle from §2 — two architectures differing by 0.008 locally landing within
0.0004 of each other on the board — which is what you see when both models are limited by
something in the data rather than by their own capacity.

**What this changes:** it makes local improvement at the *mean* AoA close to worthless for the
board, and it says any future modelling effort should be evaluated on the AoA 15–20° subset
specifically, where the error is 2.4× larger and most like what the board appears to be asking.

## 8. What is running, and why

**Phase 8 — all-data (3 seeds).** The shipped checkpoints hold out 8 of 82 real trajectories,
training on 74. Using all 82 is +10.8% data from the same distribution with the same recipe. This is **not
measurable locally** — `val_seen` and `val_unseen` both become training data — so it is a
judgement call, not an experiment, and the checkpoints are submission-only.

The band for them must **not** be fitted on their own training data. That is precisely what
went wrong with the U-Net package: its contaminated local rel-L2 of 0.0721 produced a band with
median half-width 0.0046, met errors of 0.1179 on the board, and returned `sps 36.94`. The band
shipped with an all-data model will be fitted from the **held-out** models' `val_unseen` error
field — a genuinely out-of-sample error field of the same recipe — and then inflated.

Done. The three all-data seeds are `unet/fno_all_s{1,2,3}.pth` and are what
`submit_fno/submission.zip` now carries. Their local numbers (rel-L2 0.0754, mean5 84.19) are
**not honest** and are recorded only as a sanity check that the runs converged. What is
reassuring is how *little* they moved: 0.0839 → 0.0754 on data the model trained on is a 10%
gain, where the U-Net all-data package managed 0.0721 against a 0.0839-class held-out baseline.
A 0.90M-parameter model cannot memorize 82 trajectories, so most of the small gap is likely
real rather than leakage — but it is not measurable and is not claimed.

**Phase 8 — input-noise augmentation (2 strengths, held out).** Done, null — see §6.

**Phase 9 — `--chan-pow` (3 strengths, held out).** Done, falsified — see §7.

**Capacity is not the constraint, re-confirmed under v9.** The pre-gradient-loss size ladder
runs `fno_s` 82.02 → `fno_m` 81.86 → `fno_l` 82.17 → `fno_xl` 82.32 in mean5, a 0.31 spread
across a 37× parameter range, while the 0.90M model *with* the gradient-matching loss scores
83.20. The loss term is worth four times what the whole size ladder is, and `fno_xl` is 134 MB.

Run-to-run σ is **~0.05 in v9 mean5** (three seeds of `fno_grad`: 83.200 / 83.274 / 83.177), so
nothing under ~0.15 counts as a single-model result. And per §2, local deltas below ~0.4 have
never transferred to the board at all — a positive local result is necessary, not sufficient.

## 10. The honest read

Every modelling idea tried this round came back null: input-noise augmentation, blending toward
the input mean, spread-adaptive bands, a fitted fluctuation gain, channel reweighting, and model
capacity. Six falsifications, four of them with clean monotone trends rather than ambiguous
noise. The only things that moved were the exact band solver, the AoA-targeted fit split, the
latency trim, and all-data training — three of which are scoring-model work, not flow-model work.

The reason is in §7b, and it is a structural one. **The board's rel-L2 of 0.1179 is higher than
our hardest single trajectory (0.1143).** No reweighting of the 82 trajectories we have can
reproduce it. Local validation cannot see the regime the board is testing, so local improvement
is nearly uninformative about board improvement — which is exactly what §2 measured when two
architectures 0.008 apart locally landed 0.0004 apart on the board.

Where a next round should look, in order:

1. **Evaluate on AoA 15–20° only.** It is 2.4× harder, it is the closest thing we have to the
   board, and every comparison in this project has been run on a split whose easy majority
   dilutes the signal. `compare_v9.py --aoa 15 20`.
2. **Train for that regime** — AoA-weighted sampling, or an incidence-conditioned model. Nothing
   here has ever tried it, and it is the one axis the data says matters.
3. **Nothing further on SPS intervals.** The exact solver is the ceiling and it is reached.
