## WP-1 — An FGN-style probabilistic head.  The bet.

**Read first:** `report/directions_20260809_architecture.md` §2, and arXiv 2506.10772
("Skillful joint probabilistic weather forecasting from marginals", the engine behind
WeatherNext 2).

Replace "deterministic mean + log-MSE sigma head + fitted band" with a model that emits
**samples**, trained on **CRPS**, with the noise injected in **parameter space** (a small
Gaussian vector modulating conditional-normalization layers) rather than output space.

Why here rather than anywhere else: CRPS is a proper scoring rule for what `sps` measures;
parameter-space perturbation gives spatially coherent samples from marginal-only training; and
it costs **one forward pass per sample**, which is the only reason a generative model is
admissible at all in a latency-scored task — this project already measured PDE-Refiner at 82.53
against its own MSE control's 83.83 at 4x compute.

- [ ] **Smallest honest version first.** Keep the FNO trunk. Add a 32-dim noise vector feeding
      GroupNorm scale/bias (conditional normalization) throughout, and swap the sigma head's
      log-MSE for a CRPS loss over K samples. One held-out run. Screen it on the training curve:
      if the sample spread does not track `std_t(y)` better than the current head, stop.
- [ ] **K samples must fuse.** They share weights, so `StackedFNO` should collapse them into one
      invocation exactly as it does members — the machinery exists and is exact.
- [ ] **Then decide what to submit.** Sample mean → `prediction` (best rel-L2/MVPE); sample
      spread → the TKE recomposition; sample quantiles → the interval, replacing `fit_band.py`.
      Price the whole thing with `predict_board.py` before packaging.
- [ ] **The tension to resolve, not ignore:** the scorer computes `tke` on `prediction`, so the
      submitted array must be simultaneously close in L2 *and* correctly dispersed. `ALPHA` is
      our current point on that trade; a generative model does not dissolve it. What it should
      buy is a better-calibrated, spatially coherent sigma — which is where the +7.71 lives.

## WP-2 — Phase as a co-trained second modality

**Read first:** `report/directions_20260809_architecture.md` §3, and the WN-C paper's
"griddification" (Section 2.2) plus its Extended Data Fig. 4 ablation.

WN-C does **not** run a tracker in the loop; it co-trains the dense field with a sparse,
physically meaningful second modality and gets a measurable transfer benefit that a two-stage
"predict field, then track" pipeline does not. Our analogue is not vortices but **phase**:
shedding phase, Strouhal number, convection speed, wake width — derivable from the target
trajectory by signal processing, using no extra data. Sigma *is* the amplitude envelope, and
where in the 60–75 frame shedding cycle the 20-frame output window sits determines it.

- [ ] **Diagnostic first, no training.** Decompose the per-window error into a phase/advection
      part and an amplitude part: find the spatial shift minimizing the error, see how much it
      recovers. If a small shift buys a lot, this branch is live; if not, drop it in an
      afternoon.
- [ ] Only then: auxiliary phase/Strouhal heads, co-trained, discarded at inference.
- [ ] A **phase-aligned prediction frame** (warp to co-moving coordinates, predict, warp back)
      is the stronger and more speculative version. Nothing like it has been tried here.
- [ ] **Not** a Q-criterion or lambda-2 auxiliary loss. Divergence, vorticity, spectral and
      MVPE-probe terms were all null; only velocity-gradient matching paid (+0.74), and Q is a
      quadratic invariant of grad(u) that `--lam-grad` already partly captures.

## Explicitly rejected, with reasons — do not re-propose

* **Graph neural networks.** Wrong inductive bias for a dense uniform grid with fixed geometry.
  GraphCast/WN-C use a mesh because the Earth is a sphere; we have a 32x64 flat patch. The
  flow-reconstruction GNN in the reading list is for *sparse data in time-varying geometries*.
  Also: the capacity ladder points down, and a graph transformer costs scored latency.
  Full reasoning: `report/directions_20260809_architecture.md` §4.
* **KANO** (`Papers/Track_1/2509.16825v6.pdf`, ICLR 2026). Sells *parameter efficiency* (152
  params against FNO's 566k) — we are over-parameterized, not expressivity-limited. Its core
  move (empower the spatial basis) is what `--point-mlp` did, at −0.379. The authors concede
  "orders heavier FLOPs", and FLOPs are scored. Its benchmarks are noise-free 1-D symbolic
  recovery and out-of-distribution extrapolation; we interpolate in-distribution with an 8%
  board-vs-proxy gap. Full reasoning: `report/directions_20260809_architecture.md` §5, including
  the one cheap test of its *premise* (region-wise error maps, then a per-location FiLM) if
  anyone insists.
* **More band work.** Closed three ways.
* **More ensemble members or FNO architecture variants.** Priced; the curve is flat.
* **Capacity anywhere.** Trunk closed in round 8 and again by `--point-mlp`; variance head
  closed in round 11.
* **`lam_t` upward, and `--detach-var` paired with it.** `l_var` regularizes the TKE objective
  rather than competing with it.
* **Longer training.** 40 → 150 epochs gained +0.24 on unseen and +0.86 on seen; the gap doubled.
* **Sim pretraining.** Measured at +0.00 against a from-scratch control.
* **Longer context for sigma.** Undeployable: `predict()` sees one 20-frame window, and reading
  a sibling window breaks the permutation invariance `validate_strict.py` asserts.
* **Diffusion / PDE-Refiner.** 82.53 against its own MSE control's 83.83, at 4x the compute.
  (FGN in WP-1 is *not* this: one forward pass, no denoising chain.)
* **The window-tiling exploit.** Confirmed by the organizers as not a real score. Permanently
  out of scope.

## Standing rules

* Nothing is decided on an `--all-data` number. Five contamination artifacts in six rounds.
* Read the recipe off the shipped checkpoint's stored `config`, never argparse defaults, and
  keep a no-flags guard arm first in every queue.
* Refit everything downstream of a parameter before comparing values of it.
* Never compare a local cost against a board gain; convert through the board-time model.
* Time anything with `validate_strict.py` only on an **idle** machine — contention inflates it
  ~1.6x and the error goes straight into the recorded prediction.
* Every exactness claim about `fuse()` gets a numerical check against the sequential loop.
  Round 11's mode-padding shipped a 1.26-magnitude field error that looked entirely plausible
  until it was measured.
* Record the predicted board `final` and all five subscores before each submission.
* One submission per day, 100 per phase. **Submitting is the user's call.**
