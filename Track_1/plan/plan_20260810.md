not the model itself is now closed, and closed with measurements rather than by exhaustion.**

The package that came out of it predicts **≈80.61**. The leader is 80.79. **The remaining

## 0. The instruments round 12 inherits

Round 11's real product is not the +0.10; it is that three loose proxies became calibrated
instruments, so round 12 can price a change before building it.

* `predict_board.py` — reproduces round 10's actual board result to **−0.009 final**.
* `calib_board11.py` — inverts a board SPS to a per-element tail stretch; predicted the
  round-10 SPS to **0.05**.
* `compose_v9.py` — prices any member set in `final`, accuracy and invocations and local time
  together.
* Board time: `board ms = 4.447 + 0.324*invocations + 0.431*local ms`, four rounds, ±0.064 ms.
* Seed sigma, measured on 4 seeds: **wfinal 0.017** on the AoA 15-20 proxy, 0.030 on the full
  split, TKE 0.0028. A +0.05 arm is a 3σ result; the old "0.055 noise floor" was pessimistic.

**Use them before training anything.** And use the cheap screen: `var` is a mean squared log
error against `std_t(y)`, the very quantity sigma is judged on, so **an arm that does not move
the training `var` by epoch 50 cannot move TKE**. Round 11 spent about an hour of GPU on arms
that curve would have predicted, and then used it to cancel a fourth.

## WP-1 — A second model FAMILY in the ensemble.  Best expected value.

`compose_v9.py` says FNO-internal diversity is exhausted: five w24 seeds, a modes variant, a
w48 and a w64 differ by 0.04 in `rel_l2` between four members and seven. They are all the same
model. That is exactly the condition under which a structurally different member pays.

Round 1 measured a 1.93M MSE U-Net at **83.83 on unseen against the FNO's 83.06** — before any
of the recipe work, and the FNO has since gained the two bottleneck fixes, but the U-Net has
had none of them and was never revisited.

- [ ] Port `--var-mlp` / `--lift-mlp`-equivalent fixes to `UNetTwoHead` (its heads have the same
      linear-1x1 shape). One held-out run tells you whether the family is competitive at all.
- [ ] Price it with `compose_v9.py` **before** training an all-data version. It must clear one
      invocation (0.324 board ms = 0.016 final) plus its own forward time — at round 9's
      2.648 ms/window a U-Net costs ~0.073 final, so it has to be worth more than that, or be
      made much smaller.
- [ ] If the family is competitive but too slow, shrink it rather than dropping it: the
      question is decorrelation, not individual quality (round 8: `fno_w48` was individually
      −0.34 and still beat adding a fourth w24 seed).

## WP-2 — A DEDICATED sigma network, which is what round 11's sigma arms actually point at

Round 11's sigma result needs stating precisely, because the precise version names the fix.

Six arms, and the shape of the result is what matters:

| arm | training `var` | held-out TKE | wfinal |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--lam-v 0.05 --lam-t 1.0` | 0.494 | 0.5059 | 81.463 |
| baseline (`--lam-v 0.3`) | **0.367** | **0.4823** | **82.046 ± 0.017** |
| `--lam-v 0.6` | 0.361 | 0.4868 | 82.005 |
| `--var-hidden 256` | 0.36562 | 0.4827 | 82.038 |
| `--var-deep` | 0.36801 | 0.4836 | 82.047 |
| `--var-in-std` | 0.36796 | 0.4823 | 82.049 |

Read the first three rows together. Training `var` improves **monotonically** with `lam_v`
(0.494 → 0.367 → 0.361) while held-out TKE is **U-shaped with its minimum at the shipped 0.3**.
Fitting log-sigma better does not predict the amplitude envelope better. And the last three rows
say the readout cannot move `var` at all, whatever its shape or its inputs.

> Sigma is at a genuine **generalization** ceiling for this model class and this data — not a
> capacity ceiling, not a loss-shaping ceiling, and not obviously a shared-trunk ceiling either.

That is a weaker statement than "here is the fix", and it should be. The one structural option
the six arms do not test is a **separate trunk for sigma**: a small dedicated network whose only
job is `std_t(y)`, run alongside the field ensemble and used only to supply sigma to `compose()`
and to the band. It is worth one held-out run, but go in expecting a null — nothing above
predicts it will work, and the honest reading of round 11 is that sigma may simply be done.

- [ ] Note what this is NOT: `--lam-f 0` (round 10) trained a variance-only network *instead of*
      the shared one and was worse, because the field task is a useful auxiliary. This is an
      *additional* model, with the field ensemble untouched.
- [ ] Round 3 is the supporting evidence: post-hoc sigma calibration "helps only a **separate**
      sigma net (round 3's +0.16, Phase 3's +0.06); a well-conditioned shared head just gets
      noise". That is the same distinction.
- [ ] Budget: one invocation (0.324 board ms = 0.016 final) plus its forward time. It must
      clear ~0.03 final. Price it with `compose_v9.py` before training an all-data version.
- [ ] Screen it on the training curve first. If its `var` is not clearly below 0.36, stop.
- [ ] Free option worth checking first: it does **not** need to be width 24 or an FNO, and if it
      is width-24-shaped it fuses into the existing group for **zero** invocations.

## WP-2b — Sigma's objective: closed, do not reopen

`--lam-v 0.05 --lam-t 1.0` improved the TRAINING tke loss 11% and made held-out TKE *worse*
(0.5059 against 0.4800), losing 0.58 wfinal. `l_var` regularizes the TKE objective rather than
competing with it. This retroactively explains round 10's "tripling `--lam-t` moves TKE by
0.0001", and it closes `lam_t` upward and `--detach-var` paired with it.

## WP-3 — What one subscore point is worth, sharpened

Four board rounds give `sps` 0.139–0.321 and `time` 0.043–0.120, and only loose bounds on the
other three. That range is wide enough to change decisions: at the low end of the sps weight
the alpha-0.90 change is worth +0.086 and at the high end +0.032.

- [ ] Each new board result narrows it. Keep recording the five predicted subscores before
      every submission — that discipline is the only reason the round-9 weighting model could
      be falsified and the only thing that will pin these down.

## Explicitly not worth doing

* **More band work.** Closed three ways.
* **More ensemble members or FNO architecture variants.** Priced; the curve is flat.
* **Capacity anywhere.** The trunk was closed in round 8 and again by `--point-mlp` in round 10;
  the variance head was closed in round 11.
* **Longer training.** 40 → 150 epochs gained +0.24 on unseen and +0.86 on seen; the gap
  doubled. Never extend the schedule without a matching held-out check.
* **Sim pretraining.** Measured at +0.00 against a from-scratch control.
* **Longer context for sigma.** Undeployable: `predict()` sees one 20-frame window, and reading
  a sibling window breaks the permutation invariance `validate_strict.py` asserts.
* **Diffusion / PDE-Refiner.** 82.53 against its own MSE control's 83.83, at 4x the compute.
* **The window-tiling exploit.** Confirmed by the organizers as not a real score. Permanently
  out of scope.

## Standing rules

* Nothing is decided on an `--all-data` number. Five contamination artifacts in six rounds.
* Read the recipe off the shipped checkpoint's stored `config`, never argparse defaults, and
  keep a no-flags guard arm first in every queue.
* Refit everything downstream of a parameter before comparing values of it.
* Never compare a local cost against a board gain; convert through the board-time model.
* Every exactness claim about `fuse()` gets a numerical check against the sequential loop.
  Round 11's mode-padding shipped a 1.26-magnitude field error that looked entirely plausible
  until it was measured.
* Record the predicted board `final` and all five subscores before each submission.
* One submission per day, 100 per phase. **Submitting is the user's call.**
