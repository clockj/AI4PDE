Two new flags landed this round (`f35b95f`), both adding **no parameters** and inert in
`eval()`, so every checkpoint loads through `_fno.load_any` unchanged:

* **`--mode-drop P`** (`models_alt.py:SpectralConv2d.forward`) — per-sample Bernoulli(1−p)/(1−p)
  mask over the retained `(ky, kx)` block, shared by the `+ky` and `−ky` halves (conjugate
  views of one physical wavenumber). Verified: `p = 0` is **bit-identical** to the pre-edit
  forward (maxabsdiff **0.0** on both `models_alt.FNO2d` and `submit_fno/_fno.FNO2d`);
  `p = 0.2` in `train()` changes the output (maxabsdiff 1.76) with mean |field| preserved to
  **+3.4 %** over 32 draws; `eval()` with `p = 0.2` is bit-identical to the control (0.0e+00).
* **`--lam-tail L`** (`train2.py`, right after `l_field`) — CVaR at 10 %: per sample, the mean
  of the largest tenth of the per-element squared field errors in the same `cw`-weighted
  normalized units as `l_field`. Reported as the new `tail` column, appended after `sim` so
  the existing columns keep their names and positions. Raises `NotImplementedError` under
  `--loss rel`.

### Verdicts (plan §3 drop rule: beat the control on **sps** on BOTH splits)

* **`s3` — KEEP.** Not an arm: the third twin of the control, and its job was to measure the
  seed floor. It does (see above), and it is fractionally the best of the three seeds on both
  splits. Available as a member.
* **`wd05` — DROPPED, and cleanly.** Loses on sps on both splits (−0.07 proxy, −0.03 full),
  loses rel-L2, TKE and MVPE on both, and **fits the training set BETTER than the control**
  (0.04721 vs 0.04746). That is the drop rule's second clause hit exactly: more fit, less
  generalization. AdamW decay at 0.05 is not a regularizer here — with `--dropout 0.45`
  already zeroing 45 % of the channels, the model is not in a regime where shrinking weights
  buys anything, and 0.05 is mild enough that it mostly rescales the effective learning rate.
  **The `--wd` axis is closed at this operating point.**

is.

miss.** What U-FNO exploits locally lives in the wake, more than 12 pixels from any zero element,
in the half of the domain the mask never touches. The remaining candidates are the wake
statistics themselves (PIV processing, seeding density, interrogation-window size, shedding
regime) or plain in-distribution memorization of the 74 training trajectories' wakes — which a
local conv stack can do and a mode-truncated spectral operator structurally cannot.

the two families. The arm still runs — the hidden set's FOV is unknown either way and occlusion
augmentation is a plain regularizer — but it must be read as a regularizer, not as a fix for the
U-FNO miss.
→ **Consequence for member selection.** U-FNO's gain is concentrated at AoA 10-20 (88 % of the
reduction) and in the aft half, which is exactly the shape of the board's own error — which is
why every local instrument liked it. That the same shape did not transfer makes this a
*statistical* problem (the wake it learned is our wake), not a geometric one, and it is
consistent with plan §2's decision to stay inside the FNO family.

* **`sam05` / `sam10` = `--sam-rho 0.05` / `0.10`.** A NEW MECHANISM aimed at the error tail
  the board pays for. Q2b's `--lam-tail` wrote that tail into the objective and failed (worst
  arm of six; training field −15.6 %, held out worse on everything) — the third time the
  scorer's own functional in the loss has memorized instead of generalized. SAM attacks it
  from the other side: it does not reweight the training error at all, it takes the gradient at
  the worst point of a ρ-ball around the current weights, so the optimizer prefers **flat**
  minima. Flatness is a property of the loss surface, not of the training residual, which is
  exactly what `--lam-tail` could not be. Cost: two forwards per step, ~2× wall clock.
* **`L8_md10` = `--n-layers 8 --mode-drop 0.10`.** Q2a's `md20` was the one borderline arm —
  best rel-L2 in its table on both splits, best proxy `wfinal`, dropped only because
  full-split sps was −0.03 against a 0.05 seed floor — and its cost was TKE (+0.8 % / +0.5 %),
  which is what dropping Fourier modes at training time should cost. **The pre-registered
  hypothesis was that halving p shrinks the TKE cost faster than the rel-L2 gain**, run on top
  of L8 so a win is a win over the round's best model rather than over the control.

### VERDICTS, under the plan's drop rule (beat the comparator on `sps` on BOTH splits)

**`--sam-rho` — REJECTED, and MONOTONE in ρ.** sps −0.10 / −0.09 at ρ = 0.05 and −0.16 / −0.14
at ρ = 0.10: it fails the rule on all four cells and gets worse with more of it. `wfinal` is
−0.028 / −0.046 on the proxy and −0.019 / −0.034 on the full split, the same ordering. Both
arms fit the training set substantially worse (+6.1 % / +10.7 %) with **no held-out gain**,
which is rule 4's second clause on its own. The one thing SAM does deliver is what it promises
mechanically — it finds a different, flatter minimum, visible as a training loss that will not
come down — and **that flatter minimum does not generalize better here**. It cost ~2× wall
clock per arm (5 285 s / 5 320 s against ~4 700 s), and Q2b's `--epochs 800` already showed the
control is not under-trained, so that time is not recoverable by training the control longer.
**The tail-through-optimization idea is now closed from both sides**: `--lam-tail` wrote the
tail into the objective and memorized it; SAM changed which minimum is found and gained
nothing. The only mechanism that has ever moved the board's tail remains plain regularization
(dropout, depth).

**`--mode-drop 0.10` on L8 — DROPPED, and the pre-registered hypothesis is FALSIFIED.** Against
the L8 incumbent it is **+0.09 proxy / −0.09 full-split sps** — a split decision, both cells
above the 0.03–0.05 seed floor but in opposite directions, so it fails "beat the comparator on
sps on both splits". The hypothesis was that halving p from 0.20 to 0.10 would shrink the TKE
cost faster than the rel-L2 gain. It did the opposite: **the TKE cost did not fall** (md20 on
L4 was +0.8 % / +0.5 %; md10 on L8 is **+0.93 % / +0.32 %**) while **the rel-L2 gain did**
(md20 −1.3 % / −1.0 %; md10 −0.94 % / **−0.51 %**). The trade got worse, not better.
→ What replicates exactly is the SHAPE. Both mode-drop arms, at two p values, on two depths,
against two comparators, give the **same signature: the best rel-L2 in the table, a TKE cost,
and sps flat**. `--mode-drop` is a real regularizer — it is the only axis touching the mode
dimension, where 99.4 % of an FNO's parameters live, and it fits the training set 16.7 % worse
while improving held-out rel-L2 — and at the sixteen-round weights (rel_l2 0.578 vs tke 0.068)
its trade is favourable on paper, which is why `wfinal` picks it on the proxy. **But `sps` is
the tie-breaker and `sps` will not move**, twice now. Do not spend a third arm on p; if the
axis is reopened it needs a different question (mode-drop with the TKE term reweighted, or on
the all-data members where the ensemble may absorb the TKE cost), not another p.
