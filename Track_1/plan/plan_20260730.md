Two submissions, decomposed:

| subscore | sub 1 (MSE, no var head) | sub 2 (rel-L2 loss + var head) | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rel_l2_score` | 94.118 | 94.014 | −0.10 |

**The variance head transferred. The relative-L2 loss did not.**

`compose` preserves the temporal mean exactly, and MVPE is the time-mean at 36 probe points —

the −1.28. The only other change in sub 2 was the per-sample relative-L2 field loss, which
up-weights windows with small ‖t‖ (low Reynolds number). That is a bet on the test distribution.
Locally it cost −0.10 MVPE; on the hidden set it cost −1.28.

Second fact, from the same two rows:

| | local unseen | leaderboard | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| rel-L2 error | 0.084 | 0.125 | 1.5× |
| MVPE error | 0.077 | 0.179 | **2.3×** |
| TKE error | 0.633 | 0.646 | **1.0×** |

TKE transfers perfectly; the two mean-field metrics blow up. This is **distribution shift**, not
underfitting. The known shift is Re 2968 in the test set, below the entire training range — and
`train_sim` does not help, it spans Re 3750–27975 (adding only 15225 and 27975). Nothing in
either dataset is near 2968.

## WP-C — Per-window self-normalization

**Why.** The single largest measured effect in this whole plan is the 2.3× MVPE blow-up between
local and hidden data, and the known cause is a Reynolds number below the training range. Global
`GaussianNormalizer` statistics make Re 2968 an out-of-distribution *input*: its velocities are
~20% below the lowest training case, so the normalized field lands where the network has seen
nothing.

Self-normalization removes the axis entirely. Divide each window by its own mean streamwise
speed, predict non-dimensionally, rescale on output. An unseen Reynolds number is then not
out-of-distribution at all — only the non-dimensional wake shape has to generalize, and that
varies far less with Re than the absolute velocity does.

Measured on 30 real trajectories, after dividing each by its own mean `u`:

```
u:  mean 1.0000  std 0.5260
v:  mean -0.0022  std 0.1022
```

These are the fixed constants the network standardizes against (`MEAN_SN`, `STD_SN`), replacing
the physical-unit `MEAN`/`STD`.

### Steps

| # | change |
|---|---|
| C.1 | `self_norm()` / `self_denorm()` in `model.py`; scale `s = mean_u` per window, floored at 1e-6 |
| C.2 | `--self-norm` flag through `train2.py`, `eval_wp.py`, `submission.py`; store the flag in the checkpoint so evaluation cannot silently mismatch training |
| C.3 | train on top of whatever WP-A settles on |

**Verify locally:** `final` within ±0.3 of the WP-A model on `val_unseen`. That is the *only*
honest local test — both validation sets sit inside the training Re range, so **this work package
cannot be validated locally**. Neutral local performance plus a sound argument is the pass
condition; a local *regression* is the fail condition.

**Verify on the leaderboard:** `mvpe_score` > 91.8. This is the real test and it costs one
submission slot. There are 100 per phase.

## WP-D — Sim-only diagnostic (measurement, no submission)

**Why.** We do not know whether unseen rel-L2 0.084 is an architecture limit or a data limit.
`train_sim` is a clean CFD field with no PIV noise, 1000 frames per trajectory, 100 trajectories,
and a pressure channel. Training the identical network on sim and evaluating on held-out sim
trajectories separates the two.

**What it will and will not tell us.** Sim is a smoother, more deterministic problem, so the
network will score high on it — that alone proves nothing about real data. The informative
outcome is the *shape* of the result: if sim TKE error lands near 0.1 while real sits at 0.6, the
gap is turbulence/noise in the target, not capacity. If sim TKE is also ~0.5, the architecture
cannot represent fluctuation fields and the variance head is the wrong instrument.

### Explicitly NOT a denoising pipeline

The natural follow-on — *denoise the PIV, predict clean, add noise back* — does not survive the
arithmetic, and this WP is not a step toward it:

- **Noise is 6% of the error energy.** The measured PIV white-noise floor in rel-L2 is 0.031
  (lag-1 autocovariance: noise is 16.0% of u's variance, 10.9% of v's). Leaderboard rel-L2 error
  is 0.125. `(0.031/0.125)² = 6%`. Oracle-perfect denoising moves `rel_l2_score` 94.1 → 94.3,
  worth **+0.04 final**.
- **Adding noise back is strictly harmful.** With `t = clean + noise`, predicting `clean_pred`
  gives squared error `‖clean_err‖² + ‖noise‖²`; adding an independent noise realization gives
  `‖clean_err‖² + 2‖noise‖²`. The target's specific realization is unknowable, so the renoising
  step always doubles that term.
- **The one place noise variance must be restored is TKE**, since `TKE_target = var_clean +
  var_noise` — and it must be restored as a *variance magnitude*, not a random draw. That is
  exactly what `compose` with a variance head trained on real targets already does. This part is
  built and shipping.

**Deliverable:** rel-L2 / TKE / MVPE for a sim-trained model on held-out sim trajectories, in the
next report. No score target, no submission.

## WP-E — Physics loss terms: rejected, not attempted

**A continuity penalty driving `∇·u → 0` is not supported by this data.** Measured
(`Track_1/divergence.py`, metric `rms(∇·u)/rms(|∇u|)`, where 0 is divergence-free and ~1 means
the constraint carries no information):

| | 32×64 | native 64×128 |
|---|---|---|
| real (PIV) | 0.42 – 0.49 | 0.44 – 0.50 |
| sim (CFD) | 0.28 – 0.34 | 0.28 – 0.33 |

Even the CFD field reads 0.3 — it is a solve interpolated onto a Cartesian grid across an airfoil
boundary. And **the ratio does not change when the resolution doubles**, which rules out
discretization error: second-order central differences would drop ~4× on a smooth field. The
divergence is genuinely in the data, from out-of-plane motion (PIV is a 2D slice of a 3D flow)
and from interpolation.

A softer variant — `L_div = MSE(div(pred), div(target))`, matching the data's divergence instead
of zeroing it — was implemented and then removed before running. The objection that killed it:
**there is no reason to match a quantity the data does not satisfy and the scorer never looks
at.** The scored metrics are rel-L2, TKE, MVPE on `[u, v]`. Divergence appears in none of them,
so the term can only help by acting as a regularizer, and it is the wrong regularizer here — the
correlated part of `div(target)` is dominated by out-of-plane motion `∂w/∂z`, which is **not
observable** from the 2D velocity field the network sees. Asking the model to reproduce it is
asking it to hallucinate a third velocity component.

One supporting measurement, which came out weaker than expected and is recorded because it was
used to argue the point: 23.5% (unseen) / 24.1% (seen) of `div(target)`'s temporal variance is
white noise, against 16%/11% for the velocities themselves — differencing does amplify the noise
floor, but three quarters of the divergence field is still temporally correlated. So "matching
divergence is just fitting noise" is **not** the reason to reject it; the unobservability
argument above is.

**Momentum is out of scope** on stronger grounds: it requires pressure, which is unmeasured and
zero-filled on real data, and second derivatives on a grid that already cannot resolve first
derivatives cleanly.

Code removed: `--lam-div` from `train2.py`, `divergence()`/`DX`/`DY` from `model.py`.
`Track_1/divergence.py` stays — it produced the measurements above.

## Explicitly out of scope

- **Denoise → predict → renoise.** Killed by the 6% energy budget and the renoising arithmetic
  above. The TKE half of the idea is already implemented as the variance head.
- **Hard continuity or momentum penalties.** See WP-E.
- **Denser windows, amplitude/noise augmentation, wider models.** All measured flat or negative
  in `report_20260730.md`.
- **Diffusion.** Settled negative result from `report_20260729.md`.
- **Per-element SPS bands.** Measured to lose on the hidden set (WP-B), and the oracle ceiling is
  only +0.6 final anyway.
