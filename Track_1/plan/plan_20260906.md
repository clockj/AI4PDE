### B, C — the COVERAGE LOSS (jobs 7174674, 7174675): the one objective change never made
`--lam-cov`/`--cov-c`, new. SPS's integrand is `exp(-2h/σ)·1[|E| ≤ h]` **per element**: an
element already far outside its interval is worth exactly zero, and every gradient spent
dragging it inward is taken from elements that are nearly inside. MSE weights an element by its
own error, i.e. the opposite. The term is a Welsch/redescending M-estimator,
`cn²(1 − exp(−½(E/cn)²))`, on the same cw-weighted normalized residual `--loss mse` averages,
with `cn` the scorer's own frozen σ carried into loss units (`--cov-c` is quoted in σ). The `cn²`
prefactor makes `lim_{cn→∞} = ½·mean(d²) = ½·l_field` **exactly**, so `--lam-f`/`--lam-cov` are
given per arm and always sum to one `l_field` of small-error curvature:

* **B is the ladder** — what FRACTION of the field objective redescends: 0.5/1.0, 0.25/1.5,
  0.0/2.0 at c = 0.25σ. Keeping a convex component also guarantees a gradient at initialization,
  where every `u` error is far outside c and a pure Welsch loss can stall.
* **C is the scale** — WHERE the knee sits: c = 0.15σ and 0.40σ at the middle rung, plus
  `--lam-p 0.3` and `--lam-tgrad 0.3`, both of which were rejected under weights that have since
  been withdrawn (mvpe was priced at 0.000 and is 0.0932).

Three arguments say this is the direction. (i) sps carries 0.2502 and scores 41.3 against
`100·a` = 70.6. (ii) Round 15's board result IS the effect: capacity × dropout made rel_l2
**0.64 % worse** and sps **+1.013**, for +0.166 `final` — "design against the tail" was the
lesson and nothing was ever designed against it. (iii) Round 16's `--lam-tail` (CVaR of the
**worst** tenth) lost, and that is the opposite weighting, so its failure is evidence *for* this
direction. rel_l2 is ALLOWED to get worse here; 0.4760 against 0.2502 sets the exchange rate.
