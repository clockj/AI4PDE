# Scout memo 2026-09-17 — the benchmark paper, the published rules, the physics, and a ranked list

Scout's file, companion to `plan_20260917.md`. No GPU; nothing here has been screened. Every local number was
computed on the login node from all 82 `train_real` trajectories with the competition's own windowing
(`[::2,::2]` → 32×64, T_in = T_out = 20, stride 20) and the kit's metric definitions (`scoring.py`).

**The three things that matter, up front.**

1. **The Decision phase is not a continuation of the development board.** Both Codabench pages state that the
top 10 teams are shortlisted, their methods **re-trained from scratch by the organizers**, and the **private
test set has unseen parameters — "novel angles-of-attack / Reynolds numbers"** — and that *those* scores decide
the top 3. §2.1.
2. **The TKE subscore is already at its physical floor; 20 % is not there.** The target is a 20-sample variance.
The best *phase-blind* predictor of it — each trajectory's own long-run mean variance field, hindsight,
leave-one-out over all ~43 of its windows — scores **rel-L2 0.570**. We score **0.538**, already better. A
hindsight variance from **twice** the frames still only reaches 0.456. §3.4.
3. **No usable external data, and none is allowed.** RealPDEBench's public `foil` split is a different campaign
(98 real trajectories, Re 2968–17031, 3990 frames, 128×256) from the competition's 82 (Re 3750–26761, 868
frames, 64×128), and the rules forbid training on anything but the release. §1.3, §2.2.

## 1. The RealPDEBench paper (arXiv 2601.01829)

### 1.1 What it is, and what it found

Hu, Feng, Liu, Yan, Deng, Gao, Zheng, Zheng, Yu, Wang, Li, Ma, Zhou, Lu, Fan, Wu. v1 2026-01-05, v2 2026-02-07,
**ICLR 2026 oral**. `realpdebench.github.io` · `github.com/AI4Science-WestlakeU/RealPDEBench` · HF
`AI4Science-WestlakeU/RealPDEBench` (783 GB). **Five datasets, three tasks, eight metrics, ten baselines**, 736
trajectories: `cylinder` (PIV, Re 1800–12000), `fsi`, `controlled_cylinder`, `foil`, `combustion`. Real fluid
data is PIV in **circulating water tunnels** — consistent with §3.1 (U∞ 0.058–0.384 m/s, c ≈ 69 mm, ν ≈ 1.0e-6
m²/s).

The three tasks are *real-only* (n real samples), *sim-only* (all N simulated), and *sim-pretrain +
real-finetune*. Foil rows, Rel L2: **sim-only 0.0397–0.0745, real-only 0.0159–0.0444, pretrain + finetune
0.0145–0.0375**; WDNO 0.0444 → 0.0181 (−59 %) from sim pretraining; best finetuned 0.0145, DPOT-L-FT 0.0161.
Headline: *"pretraining on simulation + finetuning on real data often outperforms training on real data alone"*,
and real training improves Rel L2 over sim-only by 9.39–78.91 %.

> **Not a contradiction of our round-5 sim-pretraining null.** Their gain is against *real only at the same n*,
> with n a handful of trajectories and a **paired** sim/real set in the same units and the same body. We have
> 74–82 real trajectories, a 3×–19× raw unit mismatch, and already run sim as a co-training regularizer
> (`--lam-sim 0.3`) — the surviving part of the same idea. Their errors are also per-step, not over a 20-frame
> window.

### 1.2 The metrics are the competition's metrics

Nine on the site (eight in the abstract): RMSE, MAE, Rel L2, R², Update Ratio, fRMSE, FE, **KE**, **MVPE**.
**KE** = `|e − ê|`, `e = (mean_t u′² + mean_t v′²)/2` — the competition's `tke`, which the kit scores as the
relative L2 of `0.5·(var_t u + var_t v)`. **MVPE** = "differences between the time-average velocity field of
real-world data, simulated data, and model predictions" — the kit's per-x-station relative L2. The competition's
five subscores are this suite plus `time` and `sps`. Our reconstruction is verified to 2.4e-6.

### 1.3 Overlap with the competition's training data — none found

**Competition `train_real` (read 2026-09-17).** 82 trajectories, `dt = 0.020 s` in all 82 (50 Hz), 868 frames in
79 (607 / 492 / 282 in three), grid `(T, 64, 128)` — geometry in §3.1. The `re` dataset inside each file is
**not** the filename: `16500_*` → 16534, `10125_*` → 10142, `17775_*` → 17812. Nominal Re grid: 3750 → 26700 in
steps of 1275 (18 values; 15225 absent). AoA 0/5/10/15/20. **The 8 holes:** (3750,15) (17775,0) (22875,5)
(22875,20) (24150,5) (25425,5) (26700,5) (26700,20).

**Public benchmark `foil` (HF, read 2026-09-17).** README: "angle of attack α: 0°–20°; Re: 2968–17031"; **98
real** trajectories, 99 numerical, **3990 frames** each, **128×256** grid, real fields `u, v`, numerical `u, v,
p`. The split indices are public and tiny — `remain_params_real.json` (78), `in_dist_test_params_real.json`
(10), `out_dist_test_params_real.json` (10) — with a Re grid of step **781.25** (2968, 3750, 4531, 5312, …,
16250, 17031, 17812) against the competition's 1275.

**Finding: different recordings.** Different Re grid (only nominal 3750 and 17812 coincide), record length (3990
vs 868), resolution (128×256 vs 64×128) and count (98 vs 82); and the sources disagree on the body — the paper
describes a **NACA0025** hydrofoil at Re 2968–17031, while Codabench Track 1 says **"paired NACA4418 airfoil
flow data"** (as does our `CLAUDE.md`). The dataset appendix would not render; treat the section name as
unresolved.

**Fairness note for the user, not a lever.** The benchmark's out-of-distribution real test list includes
`17812_15.0.h5`, and the competition's `17775_15.h5` carries `re = 17812`. If the two campaigns are the same rig
and body, a nominally held-out benchmark condition sits inside the competition's public training set — worth
raising with the organizers, nothing more. **We will not touch it:** §2.2 forbids training data that does not
trace back to the competition release, and the private set is *novel* AoA/Re, which the benchmark's 0/5/10/15/20
grid cannot supply.

* The development `final` is a **qualifier, not the prize metric** — it puts us in the top 10 and picks the

remaining ten slots are worth more as *generalization* experiments.

`.pth` that exists only in `Track_1/unet/` is a liability; name it a work item.
* **"Novel AoA / Re" reopens an arm we closed.** R14's "the hidden set is in the training speed range" is about
the **public validation set**; the private set is described the other way. §4.2.

and hyperparameters ≥ 2 weeks before the talk; code + fact sheet 2026-11-25; talk 2026-12-06.

## 3. The physics of this flow, with numbers computed from the data

### 3.1 The rig

| quantity | value | how |
|---|---|---|
| frame interval | **0.0200 s** (50 Hz), identical in all 82 files | `median(diff(t))` |
| record length | 868 frames = 17.36 s (79 of 82) | `u.shape[0]` |
| FOV | **0.21728 m × 0.10778 m**, `dx = dy = 1.7108 mm` | `x`, `y` datasets |
| grid | 64×128 → `[::2,::2]` → 32×64 at 3.42 mm | kit |
| free-stream U∞ | **0.058 m/s (Re 3750) → 0.384 m/s (Re 26761)**, linear in Re | mean `u`, top 3 rows |
| chord c | **≈ 69 mm** (median 0.0693, IQR 0.0671–0.0700) | `Re·ν/U∞`, ν = 1.0026e-6 (water, 20 °C) |
| chord, independent check | ≈ 68 mm | the exactly-zero airfoil footprint, ~40 of 128 columns |
| FOV in chords | **3.15 c streamwise × 1.56 c cross-stream** | |

The airfoil sits at the **upstream-left corner** (exactly-zero rows ~23–39, columns 0–42 of 64×128; a second
zero blob at the bottom-left is the laser shadow), and row 0 is the *top* of the FOV. The wake probes (x ∈
{13,21,29,37} on 32×64) are therefore at **0.9 c, 1.4 c, 2.0 c and 2.5 c from the leading edge** — the last two
1–1.5 c behind the trailing edge.

### 3.2 Shedding: frequency, Strouhal number, cycles per window

dominant peak above 0.3 Hz. `h = c·sin α + 0.18c·cos α` is the projected height of an 18 % thick section.

| AoA | f_s (Hz) | period, **frames** | **cycles per 20-frame window** (min–max, median) | St median | narrowband median |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.81 – 8.87 | 5.6 – 62 | 0.32 – 3.55, **1.84** | 0.295 | 0.39 |
| 5 | 0.81 – 8.76 | 5.7 – 62 | 0.32 – 3.50, **1.34** | 0.315 | 0.20 |
| 10 | 0.40 – 3.92 | 12.8 – 124 | 0.16 – 1.57, **0.63** | 0.202 | 0.19 |
| 15 | 0.75 – 4.09 | 12.2 – 67 | 0.30 – 1.64, **0.97** | 0.305 | 0.25 |
| 20 | 0.35 – 2.30 | 21.7 – 145 | 0.14 – 0.92, **0.56** | 0.229 | 0.20 |

Pooled **St median 0.276, 10–90 % 0.181–0.349** — textbook bluff-body Strouhal scaling on the projected height,
which confirms the tone is wake shedding and not a facility mode.

**Headline: 20 frames = 0.14 to 3.55 shedding periods, median 0.86, and 56 % of the trajectories see less than
ONE cycle per window.** The input window is the same 20 frames, so the input often holds less than one period of
the oscillation the output must continue.

### 3.3 Coherent versus broadband — the honest split is ~20 / 80

* **Full-record narrowband fraction** (wake-probe `v` PSD above 0.3 Hz within ±10 % of f_s): **median 0.21,
range 0.05–0.82** — high only where the wake is genuinely periodic (0.82 at Re 5028/0°, 0.76 at Re 6306/0°) and
0.14–0.40 everywhere above Re 16000.
* **20-frame window fit** to `{cos, sin}` at f_s and 2f_s (window mean removed): median 0.93 at AoA 10–20.
**Largely an artefact.** A generic rank-4 *smooth* basis (four zero-mean Legendre polynomials, no frequency
information) explains **0.813** of the same variance and a rank-2 one 0.550 — a window shorter than a period
cannot tell a sinusoid from a trend. The excess over the generic null is ~0.12, the same fact as `--tbasis 4`
matching `ozdo` (ROADMAP P3 §30).

| contrasting cases | f_s | frames/cycle | cycles/window | narrowband | sinusoid fit | lag-20 field corr. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Re 5028, AoA 0 | 1.38 Hz | 36.2 | 0.55 | **0.82** | 0.974 | −0.67 (still phase-locked) |
| Re 26761, AoA 0 | 8.87 Hz | 5.6 | 3.55 | **0.24** | 0.350 | ≈ 0 |
| Re 25482, AoA 20 | 2.30 Hz | 21.7 | 0.92 | **0.17** | 0.908 | +0.16 |

The last column is the correlation of the whole wake-box `v`-fluctuation field with itself 20 frames later:
**above Re ≈ 14000 it is within ±0.3 of zero at lag 20 and within ±0.06 at lag 40.** The horizon is at or past
the decorrelation time for most of the dataset. Two external corroborations: arXiv:2606.21538 (Guan et al. 2026,
with a RealPDEBench co-author) fits `R²(t_p) = exp(−t_p/τ)` for a 3-D cylinder at Re 2000–4000, peak **R² 0.887
at one convective time** decaying thereafter, with **τ falling as Re rises**; and arXiv:2606.10848 (Renn et al.
2026), the closest published study — FCN, U-Net, FNO and DMD trained on real PIV cylinder wakes at Re 230–2920 —
finds meaningful skill only **below ~5 steps ≈ 1/6 of a shedding cycle**, with high-frequency content gone in
the first few steps.

### 3.4 Why TKE is stuck at 54 %, and what a 20 % cut would require

The scored target is the per-pixel variance of the 20 output frames — **a 20-sample statistic of a signal whose
correlation time is a large fraction of the window**, so it is itself mostly sampling noise. Pooled over all
3465 windows of all 82 trajectories:

| predictor of the target's 20-frame variance field | TKE rel-L2 |
|---|---|
| the *previous* window's variance field (persistence) | **0.803** |
| the same, optimally rescaled (c = 0.73) | 0.722 |
| **the trajectory's own long-run mean variance field, hindsight, leave-one-out** | **0.570** |
| our shipped package (board) | **≈ 0.538** |
| the same hindsight estimate of a **40-frame** variance | 0.456 |
| … of an **80-frame** variance | 0.358 |

Per AoA the hindsight floor is 0.520 / 0.554 / 0.608 / 0.558 / 0.607 at 0/5/10/15/20; 0.600 at Re < 12000
against 0.538 at Re > 18000. The 20 → 40 → 80 sequence falls as ≈ `n^-0.35`, the signature of a
sampling-noise-dominated estimator with correlated samples.

1. **We already beat the best phase-blind hindsight predictor** (0.538 < 0.570): we extract the amplitude
information in the input window plus a little phase.
2. **A 20 % cut (0.538 → 0.43) is below what a hindsight variance from twice the frames achieves (0.456).** No
better *amplitude* prediction reaches it, not even an oracle over the trajectory; it needs the phase-resolved
fluctuation of those specific 20 frames — and §3.2–3.3 say 56 % of the data holds less than one cycle per window
and only ~20 % of the energy is at the tone.
3. **This explains the five null σ arms and the ALPHA plateau.** ROADMAP P3's "oracle σ +4.25" is an oracle over
a quantity that is ~57 % irreducible sampling noise, so the learnable part was always small — the arms were
fine, the target was not. And `ALPHA = 0.60` on a flat 0.55–0.70 plateau is exactly the shrinkage a
Bayes-optimal predictor applies to a noisy variance target. **σ-amplitude modelling should now be closed on
physical grounds, not only empirical ones.**

### 3.5 The rest of the error budget

| measured on all 3465 real windows | value |
|---|---|
| fluctuation share of the target field **energy** | **0.86 %** (the mean flow is everything) |
| ‖fluctuation‖ / ‖target‖ = rel-L2 of a *perfect-mean, steady* prediction | **0.0842** |
| our board rel_l2 error | 0.107 |
| copy the input window verbatim | rel 0.1545, tke 0.803, mvpe 0.1123 |
| input-window time-mean as a mean predictor | mean-field rel-L2 0.0896, MVPE 0.1123 |
| documented held-out anchors (kit) | persistence .138/1.000/.141; input-mean .134/—/.124 |

With ROADMAP's 70.6 % fluctuation share of squared error our fluctuation error norm is `sqrt(0.706)·0.107 =
0.0895` = **1.06× the fluctuation's own norm (0.0842)**, implying a prediction/target fluctuation correlation
**ρ ≈ 0.44** at unit composed amplitude (`diag_spectral.py` reads Γ² = 0.3865 → ρ = 0.62 on the ensemble; same
ballpark): we over-amplify past the rel-L2 optimum on purpose, to buy `tke` and `sps`, correctly signed given
the weights. And the **entire** rel-L2 prize in the fluctuation is 0.0842 of relative norm against a mean part
of `sqrt(0.294)·0.107 = 0.058` — so a 5 % relative improvement of the *time-mean* is ~1.5 % of rel_l2 ⇒ **+0.05
`final`**, the same order as anything in the fluctuation for far less work.

## 4. Ranked shortlist — none on the closed list, each ≤ 2 days on our stack

Priced against `plan_20260917.md` §1 (rel 0.4775 · tke 0.1013 · mvpe 0.0920 · time 0.0965 · sps 0.2501;
containment +10 % relative = +1.05; 5.1 → 2.5 board ms = +0.21 T1 / +0.27 T2). §3.4 forces a re-ranking:
**TKE-targeted arms drop; latency, containment and Decision-phase robustness rise.**

| # | arm | target | expected `final` | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | distillation → one model, **band re-fitted on the student** | latency | **+0.20 T1 / +0.27 T2** | in flight |
| 2 | AoA/Re-holdout instrument + re-open `BLEND_BETA` | **the private test set** | ~0 on the dev board | 1 day |
| 3 | fused-spectral efficiency → F-FNO on Track 1 | latency + containment | +0.13 T1 | in flight |
| 4 | the input window's own temporal std as a 2nd band regressor | containment | 0 … +0.20 | **1 hour** |
| 5 | repulsive / negative-correlation member training | shared error + containment | 0 … +0.10 | 2 days |
| 6 | out-of-fold residual corrector, fused | shared error | 0 … +0.05 | 1.5 days |
| 7 | binned-spectral-power loss + high-frequency scaling | tke + containment | 0 … +0.06 | 2 days |
| 8 | per-σ-decile composition exponent α(·) | rel/tke trade | 0 … +0.03 | 0.5 day |

**1 — Distillation, and the one thing that can spoil it.** Mechanism and ship rule: plan §2A. The latency
precedent is strong — TREX (arXiv:2608.11937) distils Poseidon-L into a TFNO, **269.4 → 22.1 ms per step
(12.2×)**. **The warning is about `sps`.** Across the best-quantified 2025–26 sources a student keeps
essentially all of the ensemble's regression accuracy but **loses most of its interval quality**:
arXiv:2510.19290 reports 95 % coverage **0.961 → 0.924** and **0.952 → 0.929** with ECE 0.9 % → 2.79 % (and ECE
1.46 → 6.71 in arXiv:2511.13766). Containment is a quarter of our weight, so a student that copies the mean and
σ but loses coverage hands the latency win straight back. **Two mitigations:** (a) **re-fit the band on the
student's own twins — never inherit the teacher's band**; (b) distil against a *proper scoring rule* rather than
the teacher's mean and spread — energy-distance distillation of GenCast (arXiv:2608.27728) compressed 39 NFEs to
**1** and **preserved the spread–skill ratio**, where consistency- and distribution-matching students were
under-dispersed.

**2 — An AoA/Re-holdout instrument, and re-open the mean-blend (§2.1).** The private test is *"novel
angles-of-attack / Reynolds numbers"*, so R14's verdict does not cover it. (a) An **AoA-holdout split** in
`kfold.py`: train 0/5/15/20 → test 10 (interpolation), train 0/5/10/15 → test 20 (extrapolation) — the only
instrument that sees the failure mode the prize is decided on. (b) **`BLEND_BETA`.** `mean_blend_re.py` already
measured it: in distribution the blend loses (round 6, MVPE 0.0698 → 0.1240); out of distribution it wins —
`relo` rel-L2 0.1080 → 0.1035 and MVPE 0.1135 → 0.1072, `rehi` 0.1137 → 0.1120, flat over β_eff 0.14–0.20 —
because "out of distribution the fluctuation transfers and the mean field does not", and the input window's mean
is a *measurement*. It ships at 0.0 behind a speed gate; for the Decision phase that gate should also key on
**AoA novelty**, or run ungated at small β. *Risk:* none to the dev board (default off).

**3 — Fused-spectral efficiency, then F-FNO on Track 1 (plan §2B/C).** T2R13 bought **+0.302 sps** of
containment from the F-FNO family — the only mechanism that has moved containment in 27 rounds — and the blocker
is the fixed ~27 % fused-stack penalty at b64. Two 2026 results name the mechanism. arXiv:2608.30070 measures
**five** sparsity/compression routes on FNOs and **none** reduces wall-clock (block-diagonal spectral channels:
83 % fewer parameters, 1.852 ms vs 1.397 ms dense — *slower*): "sparsity is not speed without a matching
execution path", which is exactly our symptom. What *does* work is kernel fusion: **TurboFNO**
(arXiv:2504.11681, SC '25) fuses FFT–GEMM–iFFT into one CUDA kernel for **avg 67 %, up to 150 % speedup on an
A100 2-D FNO**.

**4 — A second band regressor: the input window's own per-pixel temporal std.** The band is `half =
base(t,y,x,c)·clip(σ/σ_ref, 0.3, 3)^0.45` and keys on one signal. The input window's own per-pixel temporal std
is free (already computed for `compose`), clean (window *k*'s own input) and independent information about the
local turbulence level. Fit `half = base·(σ/σ_ref)^p·(s_in/s_ref)^q` — two scalars in `fit_band.py`, an hour of
CPU, inference-only. Containment is +1.05 per 10 % relative, so a 2 % gain is +0.21; **if `q` fits to ~0 the
band is confirmed saturated and the ninth attack can be the last.** Not the closed "spread-adaptive" band, which
used *member* spread (0.451–0.469 against the σ head's 0.329, round 12).

**5 — Repulsive / negative-correlation member training.** The only arm aimed squarely at *shared* error: a
function-space repulsion between members (kernelized on their predictions, or the negative-correlation penalty
`λ·(f_k−f̄)·Σ_{j≠k}(f_j−f̄)`), so their errors decorrelate and the spread becomes a second σ for the band.
**Design constraint, from the negative result in arXiv:2412.15758: repulsion evaluated on the training samples
degrades accuracy and underfits — it must be evaluated on unlabeled or augmentation-perturbed inputs.**
Modality-matched: arXiv:2604.17156 evaluates repulsive ensembles on turbulent cylinder flow at **Re 3900 DNS and
Re 10000 experimental PIV** and finds them competitive with HMC Bayesian PINNs at far lower cost. Screen at
**K=4 only** (round 10's dilution); the sequential variant (member k penalized against frozen 1..k−1) costs one
run. *Risk:* "learner collusion" (arXiv:2301.11323) — jointly trained ensembles inflate the ambiguity term
instead of diversifying.

**6 — An out-of-fold residual corrector.** Train one small network on the *ensemble's* residual, computed out of
fold (`kfold.py --split re` already produces exactly this); anything it finds is shared error by construction.
Fuse it into the existing invocation. **This has now been run at scale in our model class:** *Operator Boosting
Produces Pareto-Efficient PDE Surrogates* (arXiv:2606.17460) **21 of 30 dataset×architecture pairs positive, 17
with confidence intervals above zero**, Pre-register: ship only if `ens_v9` at K=4 moves ≥ 2σ of the seed
spread.

**7 — Binned spectral power loss, plus high-frequency scaling.** Both are cheap, both leave inference cost
essentially unchanged, and **neither is the L2 spectrum-matching penalty we closed**. *BSP* (arXiv:2502.00472,
*JCP* 114866 (2026), code released) bins |FFT|² into wavenumber shells and penalizes the **ratio** mismatch, so
low-energy high-k bins weigh as much as the energetic scales: Kuramoto–Sivashinsky RMSE 0.211 → 0.139 (34 %)
with spectrum rel-RMSE **2283 → 0.364**, — on point — a **NACA0012 wake LES at Re 23 000** case. *HFS*
(arXiv:2503.13695) rescales the DC and high-frequency parts of conv feature maps with learnable gains at
**+0.018–0.045 % parameters** . **§3.4 caps all of this**: the phase-blind TKE floor is 0.570 and we are at
0.538, so the prize is containment and a little `tke`, not +0.60.

**8 — Per-σ-decile composition exponent.** `compose` uses one scalar `ALPHA = 0.60`; the rel-L2 optimum is the
local correlation ρ and the `tke` optimum is 1, and ρ varies strongly across the field (§3.3). Fit α per
σ-decile (10 scalars) on the twins against the weighted objective, refit the band. *Risk:* the scalar is a
±0.006 plateau over 0.55–0.70, so the honest prior is ~0.

**Considered and rejected against the closed list.** Per-element band solvers, trajectory-CV bands, asymmetric
bounds, online calibration (closed 8×; §3.4 says the per-element error scale *is* σ); a lead-time-dependent band
(`base` already has 20×32×64×2 free parameters); member-spread σ; a frequency/phase feature from the input
(`--tbasis 4` matched window-adaptive sinusoids, §30; oracle Re/AoA conditioning removes no error); long
context, trajectory history, diffusion, DeepONet, CNO, Transolver, AFNO, a third ViT, U-FNO, sim pretraining,
`--norm self`. **Track-2 TTA stays closed:** ST-TTC (NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight, arXiv:2506.00635) buys **0.41–1.47
% MAE**; SATTS (arXiv:2602.15820) 1–3 % at 1.88× runtime and finds Tent *actively harmful* on high-dimensional
field regression; COSA (ICLR 2026) reports 14–17 % but on strongly non-stationary traffic/energy streams, and
our own oracle per-trajectory additive correction is already measured at **1.75 % of rel-L2**.
Autoregressive-rollout work — INC (2511.12764), HERO (2607.29135), Thermalizer (2503.18731), commutativity
regularization (2605.08856), memory-conditioned flow matching (2602.06689), Turb-L1 (2505.19038) — dominated the
2025–26 literature and is **inapplicable**: we predict 20 frames in one shot, teacher-forced, so nothing
compounds.

## 5. For a fluid dynamicist (plain language)

**What the score rewards.** We are given 0.4 seconds of a PIV movie of the wake — 20 frames at 50 Hz on a 32×64
grid covering about three chords behind a 69 mm airfoil in a water tunnel at chord Reynolds numbers from 3,750
to 26,800 and angles of attack from 0° to 20° — and we must produce the next 0.4 seconds. The score is a
weighted sum of five things: how close the predicted velocity field is to the measured one point by point (about
half the weight); how close the predicted *map of velocity fluctuation energy over those 20 frames* is; how
close the predicted time-averaged velocity profiles are at four downstream stations; how fast the model runs;
and how often a predicted error bar actually contains the measurement, with narrow bars rewarded. That last one
is a quarter of the weight and the one we understand least.

**Why the fluctuation is the bottleneck.** The mean flow is 99 % of the field energy and we predict it well;
everything hard lives in the 1 % that fluctuates. Two measured facts make that 1 % nearly unreachable. First,
**0.4 s is not long compared with the shedding period**: across the 82 recordings a 20-frame window spans 0.14
to 3.6 shedding cycles, median 0.86, and in 56 % of them the window holds *less than one whole cycle* — so the
model must continue an oscillation of which it has seen less than one period, at a Reynolds number and incidence
it can only infer from that same clip. Second, **the wake is mostly not periodic**: only about 20 % of the
fluctuation energy at the wake probes lies in a narrow band around the shedding tone (82 % at Re 5,000 and 0°,
but 17–24 % above Re 25,000), and the field decorrelates from itself within about 20 frames above Re 14,000.
That is why our error is not a registration or phase-lag error — shifting frames recovers 0.2 % — but genuine
loss of determinism over the horizon. One consequence deserves numbers: the quantity we are scored on for
turbulence intensity, the per-pixel variance over 20 frames, is itself a 20-sample statistic, and two
consecutive 20-frame windows of the *same* recording disagree about it by 80 % in relative L2. Even the
trajectory's own long-run average variance field, computed with hindsight from the whole 17-second record, is 57
% away from any individual window's value. Our model scores 54 %. **We are already predicting that field better
than hindsight-without-phase can**, and what remains is mostly the irreducible scatter of a short-sample
variance, not a modelling failure.

**What we would ask someone who knows this flow.** (i) Is there a physical reason the *amplitude* of the
fluctuation over a 0.4 s window should be unpredictable from the preceding 0.4 s — for instance, is the
separated shear layer on a thick cambered section at 10–20° known to switch between states, or to carry a
low-frequency flapping mode well below the shedding tone, so the local turbulence level drifts on a timescale we
never observe? At our lowest Reynolds numbers the shedding period itself is 60–145 frames, three to seven times
the window, so inside a window the shedding is indistinguishable from a trend; if that is the mechanism it fixes
a floor we should stop fighting. (ii) At these Reynolds numbers, is the phase-locked part of the wake really
only about a fifth of the fluctuation energy one to two and a half chords behind the trailing edge, and does
that fraction really collapse from 0.8 at Re 5,000 to 0.2 at Re 25,000 — or is our probe placement seeing more
shear layer and less von-Kármán street than it should? (iii) Is there a known low-dimensional structure — a POD
or resolvent basis for this geometry — whose leading modes are *shared across Reynolds number and incidence*, so
that a model could predict a handful of modal coefficients well rather than a whole field badly? A *spatial*
basis with real physical content, valid across the sweep, is the one thing on this list that could change the
shape of the problem rather than the size of the error.
