## 2. WP-D — the three gating arms

Queued in `unet/queue.txt`, ~20 min each, all held-out (never `--all-data`):

    fno_vh256.pth   --var-mlp --lift-mlp --var-hidden 256
    fno_vdeep.pth   --var-mlp --lift-mlp --var-deep
    fno_vstd.pth    --var-mlp --lift-mlp --var-in-std

All three are readouts — the trunk is byte-identical — which is the side of the boundary/trunk
rule that has paid every time. All three round-trip through `_fno.load` and `fuse` bit-exactly.

`--var-in-std` is the one to watch. `var 0.334` at convergence is a mean squared **log** error:
sigma is typically wrong by a factor of 1.78. That is an information problem, not a capacity
problem, and this arm is the only one of the three that adds information — the input window's
own per-pixel temporal std, which is exactly the statistic the log-std loss targets on the
*output* window, and a quadratic of 20 frames that the trunk would otherwise have to synthesize.

A fourth and fifth arm attack the LOSS side instead of the architecture:

    fno_ltke.pth    --var-mlp --lift-mlp --lam-v 0.05 --lam-t 1.0
    fno_ltke2.pth   --var-mlp --lift-mlp --lam-v 0.15 --lam-t 0.6

Sigma's gradient is `l_var` (log-MSE, weight 0.3) against `l_tke` (the scored TKE rel-L2,
weight 0.15). Log-MSE is uniform over pixels; TKE rel-L2 is energy-weighted and only cares
about the wake. That is how `var 0.334` — sigma typically off by 1.78x — coexists with a TKE
rel-L2 of 0.47. Round 10 tripled `--lam-t` and saw nothing, but left `--lam-v` at 0.3, so the
term it was adding to was still outvoted 2:1. **Lowering `lam_v` is the experiment that was
never run.** Watch rel-L2 and sps as well as TKE: `lam_v` is what keeps sigma sane where the
TKE loss has no gradient, and both the band and `compose()` read sigma everywhere.

**Decision rule.** Baselines measured this round on 4 seeds: wfinal **82.046 ± 0.017** on the
AoA 15-20 proxy (82.063 / 82.055 / 82.023 / 82.043), 83.608 ± 0.030 on the full split, and TKE
0.4830 ± 0.0028. The often-quoted 0.055 noise floor is pessimistic — at σ = 0.017 a +0.05 arm
is a 3σ result. Score with `compare_v9.py --aoa 15 20` and again without `--aoa`; the full
split has more windows and is the better recipe discriminator, the AoA view is the better board
proxy, and an arm should move both. A winner gets a second seed before it is believed, then
**every** all-data member is retrained with the flag (~3 h).

## 4. Longer context for sigma is undeployable — dropped on that ground

`predict()` is handed 20-frame windows and nothing else. The only source of earlier frames is
the previous window in the same call, which (a) makes the submission order-dependent, and
`validate_strict.py` asserts permutation invariance to 1e-4, and (b) rests on the evaluator
tiling its windows at stride 20 — the same structural assumption as the disclosed leak. Round
4's negative `--ctx 60` result is beside the point; this arm could not ship even if it won.

## Standing rules (unchanged, all earned)

* Nothing is decided on an `--all-data` number. Four contamination artifacts in five rounds.
* Read the recipe off `fno_grad.pth`'s stored `config`, never argparse defaults, and keep a
  no-flags guard arm first in every queue. Three arms were lost to this in round 10.
* When testing a scaling/regularization parameter, **refit everything downstream of it** before
  comparing, or the baseline wins by construction.
* Never compare a local cost against a board gain. Convert through the board-time model.
* Evaluate on the AoA 15-20 held-out proxy — but it is not the authority on member count.
* Every exactness claim about `fuse()` gets a numerical check against the sequential loop. The
  mode-padding work shipped a 1.26-magnitude field error that looked completely plausible until
  it was measured.
* One submission per day, 100 per phase. **Submitting is the user's call.**
