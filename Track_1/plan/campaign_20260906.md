### 6.4 Closed this wave

* **`--loss rel`** (per-sample relative-L2 in physical units, round 2's WP1 change re-tested at
  capacity): full sps 50.25 against 50.40, proxy 43.88 against 43.97. Negative on both.
  Round 2's +0.24 was measured on a 1.9M U-Net that underfit.
* **`--cov-c 0.15`** — the coverage scale below the winning rung: proxy sps 43.97 (exactly the
  control), full 50.32. The ladder has an interior optimum at c ≈ 0.25–0.40 and 0.15 is off it.
* **Sim PRETRAIN → real fine-tune** (`simpre` 200 ep sim-dominated, then `simft` 400 ep real
  only, 61/61 tensors copied): full sps 50.41 against 50.40, proxy 43.99 against 43.97 — **null
  to two decimals, and its TKE is the round's WORST (0.5102 full)**. Sim's value is in
  CO-TRAINING, where it perturbs every gradient step, not in initialization, which anneals away
  over 400 epochs. Round 5's `--init` result replicates at capacity and with a domain map.
* **`--subpix 0.3 / 1.0`** — predicting the target at decimation phases (0,1), (1,0), (1,1) from
  the phase-(0,0) input, head stripped at save. The auxiliary IS fitted (`sub` 3.69 → 0.056 at
  λ 0.3) and the field loss improves with it (0.0378 against the control's ~0.0382), and it
  still **loses on both splits** (full sps 50.36 / 50.34 against 50.40). Sub-pixel structure is
  learnable and is not the constraint. Fourth failure of "give the trunk a physically motivated
  auxiliary target", after `--lam-p`, `--lam-t` and `--lam-tail`.

## 7. The eight-member int8 package is a WASH — and the number that said otherwise was a stale band

Job 7188065 (`r18q`) built what job 7174852 (`r18j`) could not: `build_round13.sh` had refused
r18j because five training jobs were live and `validate_strict`'s ms/window sets the recorded
`time_score`. The guard was right. What it did NOT prevent was everything downstream of it
running anyway on **stale intermediates**, and two separate defects came out of that.

### 7.1 r18j priced the eight-member package with the SIM package's band

`build_round13.sh` writes the fitted band to `Track_2/submit/band8.npz`. r18j's build exited 1
before that line, so the file still held the round-17 **sim** band — and the next line copied it
to `band_r18_L8x8int8.npz` and printed its md5 without noticing. The md5 it printed,
`2d444bab8cf15d42b3d4d14d022224f0`, is byte-for-byte `band_r17_simx4.npz`. Every SPS number in
r18j's pricing is therefore the eight-member ensemble scored through a band fitted to a
different ensemble. The correctly-built band is `d8a96dfb70452ef6c0accd1ebe89a4b8`.

| | r18j (stale sim band) | **r18q (own band)** |
|---|---|---|
| sps on the shared instrument | 40.135 → 40.382 | 40.135 → **40.216** |
| predicted `sps` delta vs T2R6 | +0.247 | **+0.081** |
| **predicted `final` vs T2R6** | **+0.039** | **−0.003** |

→ **A build step that exits non-zero must not leave its outputs readable by the steps after
it.** The guard, the `cp`, and the pricing were three independent stages and only the first
knew the build had failed. `set +e` in the job is what let the other two run.

### 7.2 With the right band, doubling the members is a wash

Priced against T2R6 at the free 20-round weights, `--local-ms 4.30`:

| subscore | Δ | × w | = final |
|---|---|---|---|
| rel_l2 | +0.020 | 0.476 | +0.010 |
| tke | +0.062 | 0.102 | +0.006 |
| mvpe | +0.023 | 0.093 | +0.002 |
| **sps** | **+0.081** | 0.250 | **+0.020** |
| accuracy | | | **+0.038** |
| **time** | **−0.427** | 0.097 | **−0.041** |
| **total** | | | **−0.003** |

**§3b's K-curve is confirmed and its consequence is inverted.** 4 → 8 members really is worth
+0.041 of accuracy — the fit was right — and the latency it costs (local 2.45 → 4.30
4060-equivalent ms, board 4.93 → 5.55) is worth −0.041. They cancel. The `validate_strict`
measurement puts the real figure a little kinder (2.014 A100 ms × r 1.850 = **3.73**, not 4.30,
which is ≈ +0.013 back), so the honest statement is **0.00 ± 0.02, a wash**.
→ **int8 packing is therefore an option that priced at zero.** Four members fit in fp16
(4 × 54.3 = 217 MB); int8 exists only to make a fifth through eighth member possible, and the
fifth through eighth member is not worth having. The work stands — the packer, the loader,
`verify/quant_identity.py` and the 232.62 MB gated archive are all correct and reusable — but
**no package should spend a slot on member count.** Round 12's "treat any future member-set
change as ~0 until proven otherwise" survives its third test.
→ The one thing it does buy: if a future recipe is worth more per member than this one, the
storage is no longer what stops it. Keep `--int8`.

### 7.3 `cpu_smoke_submission.py --zip` was a silently ignored flag

The script takes its archive from `$RPDE_ZIP` or a hardcoded default and **has no argparse at
all**, so `--zip <archive>` — passed by r18j, by r18q, and by the round-17 builds before them —
was dropped on the floor and the gate ran on a stale `submit_fno/submission.zip`. It printed
"extracted 76.4 MB zip" for a 213.9 MB archive and returned **PACKAGE OK**. A gate that cannot
fail on the wrong input is worse than no gate.
Fixed: `--zip` is a real argument, it wins over `$RPDE_ZIP`, an unknown argument now aborts,
and the resolved path is printed on its own line. Re-run on the archive that would actually be
uploaded, `track1_r18_L8x8int8_a060.zip` gates clean — 232.62 MB extracted, bounds well-formed,
`p` zero-filled, and **permutation invariance bit-exact (0.00e+00)**, where the stale run had
reported 6.82e-05.
(`cpu_smoke_submission.py` is a **Track-1** gate — it calls `predict()`. Pointing it at a
Track-2 archive raises `no attribute 'predict'`, which is correct behaviour and not a package
defect; `Track_2/validate_t2.py` is the Track-2 gate and passed all eleven of its checks on
`track2_r18_L8x8int8_a060.zip` during the build.)
