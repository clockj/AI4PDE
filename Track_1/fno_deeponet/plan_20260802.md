identical to the U-Net. Size, not architecture, was the latency problem.

## Phase 2 — physics loss terms

Applied to the best FNO, the best DeepONet **and** the U-Net control, so the effect is measured
per architecture rather than confounded with one.

Two terms, both implemented in `train2.py` and `models_alt.py`:

- **`--lam-div`** — match the **target's** divergence field, not zero. `divergence.py` measures
  `rms(∇·u)/rms(|∇u|)` at 0.42–0.49 on real PIV and 0.28–0.34 on the CFD, and 76% of it is
  correlated structure dominated by unobservable `∂w/∂z`. A `∇·u → 0` penalty would therefore
  fight the data; matching what the target actually has is the only defensible form.
- **`--lam-spec`** — match the radially-averaged energy spectrum of the fluctuation, in log
  space. Aimed at the measured amplitude deficit at high k (gain 0.54 at k=32) *without*
  demanding phase agreement, which coherence < 0.2 says is unreachable there.

Note the two architectures need different λ_spec: at initialization the term reads 1.96 for FNO
and **81.3** for DeepONet, because a rank-*p* decoder has almost no high-k energy and the log
ratio explodes. λ will be scaled per architecture, not shared.

*Verify:* each against a matched no-physics control of the same architecture. Accept only
effects > 1σ (0.040 final).

## Phase 3 — stack what already works

On whichever architecture wins Phase 1–2: decoupled variance network, post-hoc sigma
calibration, and a 2–3 model ensemble. Measured on the U-Net at +0.15, +0.16 and +2.0 subscore
respectively; the ensemble is the largest single effect found in this project.
