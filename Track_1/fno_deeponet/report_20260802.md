## 3. Phase 2 — physics loss terms

**The divergence term works, and it is the largest single-change gain in the project.**
Raw output: `phase2_results_20260803.txt`.

Both terms are implemented in `train2.py` / `models_alt.py` and applied to **all three**
architectures, each against its own matched control.

- **`--lam-div`** matches the **target's** divergence field, not zero. `divergence.py` measures
  `rms(∇·u)/rms(|∇u|)` at 0.42–0.49 on real PIV and 0.28–0.34 on the CFD, 76% of it correlated
  structure dominated by unobservable `∂w/∂z`. A `∇·u → 0` penalty would fight the data.
- **`--lam-spec`** matches the log radially-averaged energy spectrum of the fluctuation. Aimed
  at the measured high-k amplitude deficit (gain 0.54 at k=32) without demanding phase
  agreement, which coherence < 0.2 says is unreachable there.

λ_spec is scaled per architecture: at initialization the term reads **1.96 for FNO and 81.3 for
DeepONet**, because a rank-*p* decoder carries almost no high-k energy.

### Divergence matching: +0.53 on FNO, and it improves every subscore at once

`fno_div` is **86.47 — the best score in this project**, past the U-Net decoupled pair's 86.39,
from a 0.90M-parameter network at 0.115 ms/window. rel-L2 0.0986 → 0.0873, TKE 0.5360 → 0.5119
and MVPE 0.0916 → 0.0768 all improve together, which almost nothing else in three rounds has
done — every previous gain traded one subscore against another.

Its **MVPE of 0.0768 is the best measured anywhere in this project**, beating the 33M `fno_xl`
(0.0860) and the U-Net decoupled pair (0.0773). A 0.90M network with a physics term beats a
37×-larger one without.

Why it should help FNO most, as a hypothesis worth testing rather than a conclusion: the
divergence is a *gradient-domain* quantity, so matching it constrains the velocity-gradient
structure that a mode-truncated spectral operator is otherwise free to get wrong between its
retained modes. The ordering of the gains is consistent with that — FNO +0.53, U-Net +0.11,
DeepONet +0.08 — the more the architecture band-limits, the more the constraint buys.

**It matters that the term matches the target's divergence rather than driving it to zero.**
`plan_20260730.md` rejected physics losses outright on the measurement that
`rms(∇·u)/rms(|∇u|)` is 0.42–0.49 on real PIV. That measurement was right and the rejection
was too broad: it ruled out `∇·u → 0`, which would indeed fight the data, and did not consider
matching what the target actually has.

### Spectrum matching: null to negative on all three

−0.01 (FNO), −0.10 (U-Net), −0.14 (DeepONet). Predicted by the coherence spectrum in
`report_20260731.md` §1.1: above k ≈ 15 the prediction/target coherence is below 0.2, so
inserting the missing amplitude there adds energy in the wrong phase, which raises rel-L2
without helping TKE — and TKE is already handled by the variance head. Closed.

## 7. Phase 5 — it is not divergence specifically. It is *both invariants* of ∇u.

Raw output: `phase5_results_20260803.txt`. Script: `run_phase5.sh`.

Phase 2–4 established that matching the target's divergence is worth +0.58 on FNO, and read the
mechanism as *"constrain the velocity gradient that a mode-truncated spectral operator otherwise
gets wrong between its retained modes."* That reading makes a falsifiable prediction. For a
vortex-shedding wake the coherent structure is **vorticity**, not divergence — the signal is a
train of alternating vortices, and divergence is 76% unobservable out-of-plane ∂w/∂z. So if the
mechanism is about ∇u generally, vorticity should do **at least as well** as divergence, and the
full tensor — which subsumes both invariants plus the strain rates — should do **best**.

That is what happened.

| model | loss term | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | **final** | vs `fno_div_hi` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fno_div_hi` | λ_div 1.0 | 0.0862 | 0.5082 | 0.0757 | 86.53 | — |
| `fno_vort` | λ_vort 1.0 | 0.0866 | 0.5076 | 0.0752 | 86.53 | 0.0 |
| `fno_vort_hi` | λ_vort 3.0 | 0.0857 | 0.5032 | 0.0758 | 86.57 | +0.04 |
| **`fno_grad`** | **λ_grad 1.0** | 0.0839 | 0.5003 | 0.0726 | **86.68** | **+0.15** |
| **`fno_grad_hi`** | **λ_grad 3.0** | 0.0834 | 0.4985 | 0.0727 | **86.68** | **+0.15** |
| **`fno_dv`** | **λ_div 1.0 + λ_vort 1.0** | 0.0835 | 0.5028 | 0.0718 | **86.67** | **+0.14** |
| `unet_div_hi` | λ_div 1.0 | 0.0895 | 0.5436 | 0.0810 | 86.13 | — |
| `unet_grad` | λ_grad 1.0 | 0.0877 | 0.5411 | 0.0765 | 86.26 | +0.13 |

**+0.15 = 3.75σ** against the σ = 0.040 run-to-run noise, and it improves rel-L2, TKE and MVPE
*simultaneously* — the same signature that made the divergence term credible, and still the only
class of change in five rounds that does it.

### The decisive row is `fno_dv`

`fno_dv` constrains divergence **and** vorticity, with **no strain-rate terms**, and reaches
86.67 — indistinguishable from the full tensor's 86.68. Combined with either invariant alone
scoring 86.53–86.57, that localizes the effect precisely:

* **one invariant is not enough** (86.53–86.57),
* **both invariants are sufficient** (86.67),
* **the strain rates add nothing on top** (86.68, +0.01 = 0.25σ).

∇u in 2D has four components; div and vorticity are two independent scalar invariants of it.
Constraining both pins the antisymmetric part and the trace, leaving only the traceless
symmetric part free — and the data says that remaining part is where the model was already
fine. This is a statement about which part of the velocity gradient a truncated spectral
operator gets wrong, not about physics being injected.

### Three independent confirmations, not one run

`fno_grad`, `fno_grad_hi` and `fno_dv` are three separate training runs, at two different λ and
one different loss *form*, all landing at 86.67–86.68. With σ = 0.040 that is a replication, not
a lucky seed. The U-Net reproduces the ordering independently (+0.13, `unet_div_hi` → 
`unet_grad`), so it is not an FNO artifact.

### What this rules out

λ_grad 1.0 and 3.0 tie exactly. If the mechanism were "weight high wavenumbers harder," λ 3.0
would have beaten λ 1.0, and the natural next term would be a **Laplacian** match (−k² rather
than ik). It did not, so that term was **not built** — the λ plateau is the evidence against it.

### Post-hoc sigma calibration does not transfer to the FNO's own head

`fno_div_hi`'s 86.53 was measured without `eval_sweep.py --calibrate`, which defaults off — so
this looked like free points. It is not. Raw output: `phase5_calib_20260803.txt` (its `ms/win`
is contaminated by concurrent Phase 6 training; only the timing-independent error metrics below
are comparable).

| `fno_grad` | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | sps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| uncalibrated | 0.0839 | **0.5003** | 0.0726 | 62.1 |
| calibrated | 0.0840 | 0.5047 | 0.0726 | 62.1 |

Everything is unchanged except TKE, which gets **worse**. This does not contradict round 3
(+0.16) or Phase 3 (+0.06): both calibrated a *separate* sigma network (`wpF1_varonly`) paired
with a field-only model. The FNO's own variance head is already well conditioned, so the
least-squares refit adds variance rather than signal — and since sigma feeds `compose()` at
α = 0.75, that noise lands directly on TKE. **Not applied.**

## 8. Packaging — and a measurement error that inflates every local score in this project

`Track_1/submit_fno/` is the first legitimate package built in five rounds; every previous
round's best model is recorded as "not yet packaged," so none of that work has ever scored.
It wraps `fno_grad_s2` with `_fno.py`, a fitted `band.npy`, and `validate_strict.py`, which
loads `submission.py` by file location from an unrelated cwd with `scipy`/`h5py`/`einops`/
`matplotlib`/`pandas` blocked — the harshest plausible load path.

**Building it surfaced a systematic error.** `eval_wp.py` times the **forward pass only**, and
every `time_score` in every report in this project comes from that. The evaluator times
`predict()` end to end. Measured on the same 336 windows:

| what is timed | ms/window | `time_score` |
|---|---|---|
| forward pass only (`eval_wp.py`, all reports) | 0.109 | 98.79 |
| **end-to-end `predict()` (what the evaluator scores)** | **1.383** | **95.83** |

That is **−0.59 on `final`**, applied uniformly. It does not change any ranking here — every
candidate shares one architecture — but the absolute local numbers are optimistic by about half
a point, on top of the ~1.7 the hidden set already runs below local.

Most of the gap is not compute. The API mandates returning three `(N, 20, 32, 64, 3)` float32
arrays; at N = 336 that is 3 × 165 MB of writes, which is a hard floor no model change touches.
Three fixes took it from 2.425 ms to 1.383 ms (`final` 85.84 → **86.10**) with the error metrics
bit-identical:

| fix | ms/win |
|---|---|
| as first written | 2.425 |
| stop materializing `half`/`lower`/`upper` — `band` is `(1,20,…)` and broadcasts natively | 1.902 |
| write `u,v` into a pre-zeroed output; upload whole windows and slice on the GPU; batch 64 | **1.383** |

### A test that failed for the right reason

`validate_strict.py` asserts the prediction is independent of window **order** — the property
`submit_trick/` deliberately violates. It failed on the first run. The cause was *not* order
dependence: permuting perturbs the raw field by ~1e-4 because cuFFT and cuBLAS reductions are
not invariant to batch composition, and `predict_fno`'s division by `s_ + 1e-6` amplifies that
to ~1e-3 wherever the temporal std is near zero. The proof it is numerical: **batch-16 versus
batch-1 inference differs by the same ~2e-4**, and batch-1 is independent by definition. The
assertion now tests what actually matters — mean field drift < 1e-4 of rms, and all three error
metrics stable to 1e-5 under permutation.

## 9. Phase 6 — sim → real pretraining works, and still loses

Raw output: `phase6_results_20260803.txt`. Script: `run_phase6.sh`.

Every model in this project was real-only, which is a strange place for a **Sim2Real** track to
end up. `train_sim` has 100 trajectories × 1000 frames against real's 82 × 868, and covers
Re 15225 and 27975 that real does not — while the hidden test set contains unseen Reynolds
numbers.

The standing objection is that sim-only training scores 69.0, below free persistence at 79.7.
That measures **zero-shot transfer, not pretraining**: the official `sim_real_ft` checkpoints
also draw all their real skill from stage 2, and still beat their own `sim_pretrain` by 14
points.

Both stages ran in **self-normalized space** (`--norm self`), because raw sim is 3–19× real's
scale, so shared weights are meaningless in physical units. New in `train2.py`: **`--init`**,
which warm-starts from a checkpoint, copying only shape-matching tensors and printing the count
(`33/33 tensors copied`) so a silent partial load cannot make a fine-tuning run look like a
from-scratch run.

| model | normalizer | init | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | **final** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fno_grad` | real | — | 0.0839 | 0.5003 | 0.0726 | 86.67 |
| **`fno_grad_s2`** | real | — | 0.0832 | 0.4987 | 0.0724 | **86.70** |
| `fno_grad_self` | **self** | — | 0.0860 | 0.5241 | 0.0739 | 86.47 |
| `fno_s2r` | **self** | sim, lr 1e-4 | 0.0861 | 0.5119 | 0.0736 | 86.55 |
| `fno_s2r_lo` | **self** | sim, lr 5e-5 | 0.0873 | 0.5176 | 0.0745 | 86.48 |

### The control is what makes this readable

**Sim pretraining is worth +0.08** (`fno_s2r` 86.55 vs its matched control `fno_grad_self`
86.47) — 2σ, marginal but positive, and it shows up almost entirely in TKE (0.5241 → 0.5119).

**But `--norm self` costs −0.20 on its own** (86.47 vs 86.67). The bundle therefore nets out
*below* plain real-normalized training. Without the control this would have read as "sim
pretraining is worth −0.12 and is closed," which is the wrong conclusion.

`fno_s2r_lo` at the gentler lr 5e-5 is *worse* (86.48), so stage 1 is a weak prior that stage 2
must be free to move away from — consistent with sim-only scoring 69.0.

### The seed repeat holds

`fno_grad_s2` reaches **86.70** against `fno_grad`'s 86.67. Three independent runs of the
gradient recipe (`fno_grad`, `fno_grad_hi`, `fno_grad_s2`) now sit at 86.67–86.70. The +0.15
over divergence matching is not a seed artifact.

### Caveat that this phase does not settle

locally because both validation sets sit inside the training Re range while the hidden test set
does not. So the −0.20 measured for `--norm self` is a local number that may not transfer, and
round 2 already showed a 0.002 local MVPE difference becoming 1.28 on the board. Phase 7 asks
whether the pretraining gain survives without the normalizer, which is the version that does not
depend on resolving this.

## 11. Ensembling was rejected on the wrong cost model

`CLAUDE.md` and §6 both record ensembling as negative (−0.07 / −0.10) and say not to pursue it
"at this operating point." That verdict was computed with **forward-pass-only latency**, where
the forward pass was most of the cost (0.262 ms of ~0.43) and a second member nearly doubled it.

§8 changed the cost model. End to end the forward pass is 0.109 ms of 1.383, and the other
~1.27 ms is fixed API overhead — three 165 MB output arrays — which a second member **does not
duplicate**, provided members are averaged on the GPU before the single host transfer. That is
how `_fno.predict_fno` now does it: average raw field and sigma across members, then `compose()`
once on a single coherent field.

Measured end to end through `validate_strict.py`, on an idle GPU:

| members | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | ms/win | `time` | **FINAL** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (`fno_grad_s2`) | 0.0833 | 0.4987 | 0.0724 | 1.383 | 95.83 | 86.10 |
| **3 (seeds)** | 0.0807 | 0.4906 | 0.0698 | **1.190** | 96.12 | **86.30** |
| 7 (seeds + loss-form diversity) | **0.0795** | **0.4874** | **0.0689** | 1.660 | 95.45 | 86.22 |

**Three members is the operating point.** Going from 3 to 7 improves every error metric — 7
members has the best rel-L2, TKE and MVPE in the entire project — and gains **+0.30 across the
four accuracy subscores, which the latency then removes exactly (−0.32 on `time`)**. The curve
is flat from 3 to 7, so 3 wins on size (11 MB vs 26 MB) and on having fewer things to go wrong,
not on score.

That flatness is the same phenomenon that has shaped this whole project: at e ≈ 0.08 the
`100/(1+0.5e)` curve is nearly flat, so accuracy is cheap to the score and latency is not.
Ensembling only became worthwhile once the *fixed* part of the latency was large enough to
amortize — which is a fact about the API, not about the models.
