# FNO / DeepONet + physics losses on real data — 2026-08-02

Goal as set: **real-data local score above 92**, restricted to FNO or DeepONet, with physical
loss terms, applied also to the current best model. Plan: `plan_20260802.md`.

Env: `realpde` (torch 2.2.2+cu121) · RTX 4060 8 GB · 74 training trajectories, 8 held out
Evaluation: `val_unseen.npz` (336 windows), SPS band `c × optimal h(RMSE·|w|)` fitted on
`val_seen.npz`. Run-to-run noise on `final` is **σ = 0.040** (`report_20260731.md` §2.4).

The control reproduces at 86.05 against 86.08 measured yesterday, a 0.75σ difference, which

### FNO capacity is flat across a 37× parameter range

85.87–85.94 from 0.90M to 33.59M — a 0.07 spread against σ = 0.040. This is the fifth
independent confirmation that capacity is not the binding constraint here (U-Net width 96 in
round 1, variance-net width 64 in round 3, long context on sim, and now FNO across 37×).

Field quality *does* improve with size: `fno_xl` has the best rel-L2 (0.0954) and MVPE (0.0860)
of **any model in this project, the U-Net included**. It is latency that eats the gain
(`time_score` 98.0 vs 98.8). That is a real finding for Phase 3, where a separate sigma network
means field quality is the only thing the field net has to deliver.

### DeepONet is limited by its parameterization, not by training

81.09 → 84.30 is a large gain over the published baseline, but it plateaus 1.75 below FNO and
its MVPE is ~55% worse (0.1345 vs 0.0867). On a fixed output grid a DeepONet is a rank-*p*
linear decoder over a condition-independent basis; `report_20260729.md` predicted exactly this
failure mode, and tripling *p* from 128 to 512 moves MVPE only 0.1644 → 0.1345. It will not
reach the other two.

## 3. Phase 2 — physics loss terms

**The divergence term works, and it is the largest single-change gain in the project.**
Raw output: `phase2_results_20260803.txt`.

Both terms are implemented in `train2.py` / `models_alt.py` and applied to **all three**
architectures, each against its own matched control.

- **`--lam-div`** matches the **target's** divergence field, not zero. `divergence.py` measures
  `rms(∇·u)/rms(|∇u|)` at 0.42–0.49 on real PIV and 0.28–0.34 on the CFD, 76% of it correlated
  structure dominated by unobservable `∂w/∂z`. A `∇·u → 0` penalty would fight the data.
- **`--lam-spec`** matches the log radially-averaged energy spectrum of the fluctuation. Aimed
  at the measured high-k amplitude deficit (gain 0.54 at k=32) without demanding phase
  agreement, which coherence < 0.2 says is unreachable there.

λ_spec is scaled per architecture: at initialization the term reads **1.96 for FNO and 81.3 for
DeepONet**, because a rank-*p* decoder carries almost no high-k energy.

| run | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | **final** | Δ vs control | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fno_s` — control | 0.0986 | 0.5360 | 0.0916 | 85.94 | — | |
| **`fno_div`** | **0.0873** | **0.5119** | **0.0768** | **86.47** | **+0.53** | **13σ, real** |
| `fno_spec` | 0.1043 | 0.5194 | 0.0925 | 85.93 | −0.01 | null |

| `don_spec` | 0.1332 | 0.6415 | 0.1507 | 84.16 | −0.14 | negative |

### Divergence matching: +0.53 on FNO, and it improves every subscore at once

`fno_div` is **86.47 — the best score in this project**, past the U-Net decoupled pair's 86.39,
from a 0.90M-parameter network at 0.115 ms/window. rel-L2 0.0986 → 0.0873, TKE 0.5360 → 0.5119
and MVPE 0.0916 → 0.0768 all improve together, which almost nothing else in three rounds has
done — every previous gain traded one subscore against another.

Its **MVPE of 0.0768 is the best measured anywhere in this project**, beating the 33M `fno_xl`
(0.0860) and the U-Net decoupled pair (0.0773). A 0.90M network with a physics term beats a
37×-larger one without.

Why it should help FNO most, as a hypothesis worth testing rather than a conclusion: the
divergence is a *gradient-domain* quantity, so matching it constrains the velocity-gradient
structure that a mode-truncated spectral operator is otherwise free to get wrong between its
retained modes. The ordering of the gains is consistent with that — FNO +0.53, U-Net +0.11,
DeepONet +0.08 — the more the architecture band-limits, the more the constraint buys.

**It matters that the term matches the target's divergence rather than driving it to zero.**
`plan_20260730.md` rejected physics losses outright on the measurement that
`rms(∇·u)/rms(|∇u|)` is 0.42–0.49 on real PIV. That measurement was right and the rejection
was too broad: it ruled out `∇·u → 0`, which would indeed fight the data, and did not consider
matching what the target actually has.

### Spectrum matching: null to negative on all three

−0.01 (FNO), −0.10 (U-Net), −0.14 (DeepONet). Predicted by the coherence spectrum in
`report_20260731.md` §1.1: above k ≈ 15 the prediction/target coherence is below 0.2, so
inserting the missing amplitude there adds energy in the wrong phase, which raises rel-L2
without helping TKE — and TKE is already handled by the variance head. Closed.

## 4. Phase 4 — λ_div, capacity, and the combination

Raw output: `phase4_results_20260803.txt`, `phase4_pair_20260803.txt`.

### λ_div has a shallow optimum at 1.0

| λ_div | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | **final** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (`fno_s`) | 0.0986 | 0.5360 | 0.0916 | 85.95 |
| 0.1 | 0.0899 | 0.5226 | 0.0786 | 86.34 |
| 0.3 | 0.0873 | 0.5119 | 0.0768 | 86.48 |
| **1.0** | **0.0862** | **0.5082** | **0.0757** | **86.53** |
| 3.0 | 0.0877 | 0.5070 | 0.0772 | 86.48 |

**`fno_div_hi` at λ_div = 1.0 is the best model in this project: 86.53**, a 0.90M FNO at
0.106 ms/window. The optimum is shallow — anything in 0.3–3.0 is within 0.05 — so the term is
not delicate to tune, which is what one wants from a regularizer.

The U-Net prefers a *lower* weight (λ 0.3 → 86.17, λ 1.0 → 86.13), consistent with the term
doing less work there: the U-Net's convolutional stencil already constrains local gradients,
while a mode-truncated spectral operator does not.

### Capacity, again, does not help

`fno_div_l` — the same divergence loss on the 14.18M width-48 FNO — scores **86.19**, worse
than the 0.90M model's 86.53 and worse on every field metric (rel-L2 0.0885 vs 0.0862, TKE
0.5424 vs 0.5082). Sixth independent confirmation.

### The combination does not beat the single model

`fdiv1/2/3` are field-only FNOs with the divergence loss, paired with the `wpF1_varonly` sigma
net, calibrated, and ensembled:

| configuration | ms/win | rel-L2 | TKE | MVPE | **final** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fdiv1` + sigma | 0.198 | 0.0848 | 0.5202 | 0.0751 | 86.41 |
| `fdiv1` + sigma + calib | 0.198 | 0.0851 | 0.5105 | 0.0751 | 86.46 |
| ensemble ×2 + sigma + calib | 0.302 | 0.0827 | 0.5115 | 0.0731 | 86.46 |
| ensemble ×3 + sigma + calib | 0.408 | **0.0818** | 0.5114 | **0.0723** | 86.43 |
| **`fno_div_hi` alone** | **0.106** | 0.0862 | 0.5082 | 0.0757 | **86.53** |

**Field quality keeps improving and the score does not.** The ×3 ensemble has the best rel-L2
(0.0818) and MVPE (0.0723) measured anywhere in this project — but at 0.408 ms it pays
`time_score` 97.6 against the single model's 98.8, and the `100/(1+0.5e)` curve is so flat at
e ≈ 0.08 that 0.0862 → 0.0818 is worth only +0.2 subscore while the latency costs 1.2.

This is the clearest statement of the metric's shape in the whole project: **at this operating
point, accuracy is nearly free to the score and latency is not.** The ensemble result from
`report_20260731.md` (+2.0 subscore on the U-Net) still holds in *field* terms; it simply stops
paying once the field net is already fast and accurate.

## 5. Against the goal

**Goal: 92. Result: 86.53.** Not reached, and the gap is not a tuning gap.

| | final |
|---|---|
| best before this round | 86.39 (U-Net decoupled pair + calibration) |
| **best after** | **86.53** (`fno_div_hi`: 0.90M FNO, λ_div 1.0) |
| measured forecasting ceiling | ~87.5 |
| perfect predictor under this scorer | 94.4 |

`sps_score = 100/(1+exp(−w))` with `w` a weighted mean of terms each ≤ 1 caps at **73.11**, so
92 requires the other four subscores to average 98.4 — rel-L2, TKE and MVPE all near 0.03 — while
the measured sampling floors are 0.0531 (time-mean) and ≈0.47 (TKE). No arrangement of the
models trained here reaches it.

### What this round actually produced

| change | Δ final | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **divergence matching on FNO** (λ 1.0) | **+0.58** vs `fno_s` | 14σ, the largest single-change gain in the project |
| divergence matching on the U-Net | +0.11 | 2.8σ |
| divergence matching on DeepONet | +0.08 | 2σ, marginal |
| spectrum matching | −0.01 / −0.10 / −0.14 | null to negative on all three |
| FNO capacity 0.90M → 33.6M | ±0.07 | flat |
| decoupled pair on FNO | −0.07 | neutral; FNO's own head is already fine |
| ensembling ×2, ×3 | −0.07 / −0.10 | field quality up, score down on latency |

### Two prior conclusions this round overturns

1. **FNO was recorded as dominated and not worth time. That was a size artifact.** The official
   50M checkpoint scores 83.06 at 15.5 ms; a 0.90M FNO scores 85.95 at 0.11 ms and 86.53 with
   the divergence term. The 50M model was slower *and* worse. `CLAUDE.md`'s "do not spend time
   on CNO/Transolver/FNO" was right about two of the three.
2. **Physics losses were rejected too broadly.** `plan_20260730.md` ruled them out on the
   correct measurement that `rms(∇·u)/rms(|∇u|)` is 0.42–0.49 on real PIV — which rules out
   `∇·u → 0`, and only that. Matching the divergence the target *actually has* is worth +0.58
   and improves rel-L2, TKE and MVPE simultaneously, which no other change in three rounds has
   done.

### Recommended next

- **Ship `fno_div_hi`.** It is the best model, the smallest (0.90M, 3.6 MB), and the fastest
  (0.106 ms). Add the fitted SPS band; the decoupled pair and calibration are not needed for it.
- **Re-test the divergence term on sim**, where the CFD's divergence is a cleaner target
  (0.28–0.34 against real PIV's 0.42–0.49).
- **Do not** pursue: spectrum matching, FNO capacity, DeepONet, ensembling at this operating
  point, or any further loss reweighting.
